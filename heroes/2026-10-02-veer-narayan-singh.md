Veer Narayan Singh (1795-1857) was the zamindar of Sonakhan, in what is now Chhattisgarh's Balodabazar-Bhatapara district, and is honoured as the first freedom fighter and first martyr of Chhattisgarh. A leader of the Binjhwar tribal community, he came from a family with a record of administrative leadership: his great-grandfather had served as the Dewan of Sonakhan. He inherited the landlordship from his father at the age of thirty-five and earned a reputation as a just and benevolent ruler devoted to his people's welfare, remembered in the region as the Mahanayak of Sonakhan.

His collision with the British began not with politics but with hunger. During the severe famine of 1856, while grain was being exported and hoarded even as peasants starved, Singh seized the stocks of a British-backed trader at Kashadol and distributed the grain among the starving villagers. The colonial administration treated this act of defiance as looting, arrested him, and jailed him at Raipur.

In 1857, as the great revolt swept northern India, Singh escaped from the Raipur prison with the help of Indian soldiers and returned to Sonakhan. His escape turned a local act of defiance into open revolt within months. There he raised a force of about five hundred tribal and peasant fighters and opened an armed uprising against British rule, one of the earliest organised revolts in central India. In character it was a combined peasant-zamindar rebellion, driven by anger at British revenue policy and the forcible export of grain while the countryside starved, and it drew in local zamindars and peasants alongside his own men. The British dispatched a military force under Captain Smith to crush the Sonakhan army. Singh was captured, and on 10 December 1857 he was publicly hanged at Jaistambh Chowk in Raipur.

For decades his sacrifice lived mainly in local memory, but Chhattisgarh has since reclaimed him. The state named its international cricket stadium in Raipur the Shaheed Veer Narayan Singh International Cricket Stadium, instituted the Shaheed Veer Narayan Singh Samman for work among tribal and backward communities, and issued a commemorative postal stamp in his honour in 1987. In November 2025, the Prime Minister inaugurated the Shaheed Veer Narayan Singh Memorial and Tribal Freedom Fighters Museum, described as India's first digital museum of tribal freedom fighters, at Nava Raipur Atal Nagar. From a zamindar who broke open granaries in a famine year, he has become the enduring symbol of Chhattisgarhi pride in the freedom struggle.
