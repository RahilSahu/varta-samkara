Muthulakshmi Reddi: India's first woman legislator, and the doctor who took on the devadasi system

Portrait: Wikimedia Commons, public domain

Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddi was born on 30 July 1886 in Pudukkottai, Tamil Nadu. Her father, S. Narayanaswami Iyer, was a tutor to the royal family of the princely state, and her mother Chandrammal was of devadasi descent. Defying convention, her father homeschooled her, insisted she carry the initials of both parents (breaking the devadasi custom of carrying only the mother's name), and resisted attempts to dedicate her young to the devadasi tradition. She also resisted her family's plan for an early arranged marriage, insisting on an education instead.

In 1907 she entered Madras Medical College as the first and only woman student in its surgery department. She graduated with honours in 1912, becoming one of the first women doctors in India and the first woman house surgeon at the Government Maternity Hospital, Madras. In 1914 she married Dr. Sundara Reddi on the understanding that he would treat her as an equal.

In 1918 she co-founded the Women's Indian Association. In 1926 she was nominated to the Madras Legislative Council, becoming its first woman member and vice-president, which made her the first woman legislator in British India. She was also the first woman to become deputy president of the Council and the first alderwoman of the Madras Corporation. In the Council she worked to raise the minimum age of marriage for girls, helped pass the Immoral Traffic (Suppression) Act, and led the campaign against the devadasi system, introducing a Bill against it in 1927. Two decades later the Madras Devadasis (Prevention of Dedication) Act of 1947 gave devadasi women the right to marry and made it illegal to dedicate girls to temples.

She resigned from the Council to support the Salt Satyagraha. In 1930, when three devadasi girls knocked on her door, she founded the Avvai Home to shelter and educate girls like them. She represented India at the 1926 International Suffrage Conference in Paris and the 1933 Congress of Women in Chicago. After losing her sister to cancer, she founded the Cancer Institute in Adyar, Chennai, in 1954, which became one of the world's most respected oncology centres. She received the Padma Bhushan in 1956 and died in 1968 at the age of 81.

Google marked her 133rd birth anniversary with a doodle in 2019, and the Tamil Nadu government celebrates her birthday as "Hospital Day" in government hospitals. Yet for all her firsts, across medicine, legislation and public health, she is still barely a household name.
