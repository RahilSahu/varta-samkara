---
title: "Komaram Bheem: The Gond Leader Behind Jal, Jangal, Zameen"
date: 2026-10-02
source: "The Weekly News, The Federal"
---

The slogan "Jal, Jangal, Zameen", water, forest, land, is the war cry of India's Adivasi movements. It was coined by a Gond leader who never went to school, who fought the Nizam of Hyderabad's police with bows and arrows, and who was shot dead in the forests of Adilabad in 1940. His name was Komaram Bheem, and the world learned it only when a blockbuster film borrowed his name and invented the rest.

Bheem was born on 22 October 1901 at Sankepalli, in what is now Telangana's Komaram Bheem Asifabad district, into a Gond (Koitur) family. He had no formal education. When he was about fifteen, his father Komaram Chinnu was reportedly killed by forest officials for asserting Adivasi rights. After a land conflict in which Bheem killed the jagirdar Siddhiki, he fled the region and worked in Assam's tea and coffee plantations from roughly 1923 to 1928, where he saw plantation labour unrest firsthand. He returned to Adilabad in 1928 and began organizing Gond communities.

The grievances were concrete: the Nizam's state allotted forest land to outsiders, restricted podu shifting cultivation, let forest officials and revenue agents extort and assault tribals, and taxed crops grown on land the Gonds considered their own. Bheem first tried petitions and negotiation; when those failed, he and allied Gond leaders chose armed resistance. From bases at Babejheri and Jodeghat, a small armed force of Gond fighters waged a guerrilla campaign through the 1930s, ambushing police parties and revenue agents. The Nizam offered land grants to settle the matter; accounts say Bheem rejected them and demanded regional autonomy for the Gonds, the eviction of forest officials and zamindars, and the release of Gond prisoners. It was in this struggle that he raised the slogan Jal, Jangal, Zameen, asserting that the people of the forest owned the forest's water, woods and soil.

The end came by betrayal. According to later accounts, a man named Kurdu Patel disclosed Bheem's whereabouts, and in October 1940 the Nizam's armed police surrounded his hideout at Jodeghat and shot him dead. The date most commonly given is 27 October 1940, though some accounts record 19 October.

Bheem was deified as a pen, a spirit, in Gond culture, and his death anniversary is observed by Gonds at Jodeghat. In 2016 Telangana named a district after him. Beyond the region he was barely known until the 2022 film RRR paired him with Alluri Sitarama Raju in a fictional friendship; the director himself admitted the two men never met in real life. Bheem is forgotten nationally because he left no documents, only oral memory, and because his war was against a princely state, not the British, placing him outside the standard freedom-struggle syllabus. His slogan, though, outlived every account of him.
