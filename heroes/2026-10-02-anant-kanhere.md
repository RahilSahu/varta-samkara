Anant Laxman Kanhere (7 January 1892 - 19 April 1910; a few accounts give his birth year as 1891) was eighteen years old when he shot dead A. M. T. Jackson, the British collector of Nashik, an act that shook the colonial administration and marked a turning point in Maharashtra's revolutionary movement. Born in Aayani (Anjani) village of Khed taluka in Ratnagiri district into a Chitpavan Brahmin family, he was educated at Nizamabad and Aurangabad, where he came into contact with secret revolutionary groups, including the Abhinav Bharat Society founded by the Savarkar brothers.

Jackson was an unusual target. A scholar of Sanskrit known locally as "Pandit Jackson," he enjoyed a reputation as a sympathetic and cultured officer, and many Indians could not understand why he was marked for death. The revolutionaries' reason was political: Jackson had been instrumental in the arrest and prosecution of Ganesh Damodar Savarkar, known as Babarao, for his seditious writings and revolutionary organising. A Nashik group led by Krishnaji Karve resolved to eliminate the collector. The plan was set for January 1910, but Jackson was then promoted to Commissioner of Bombay, so the conspirators moved quickly: Nashik's citizens had arranged a farewell for him at the Vijayanand Theatre, where the Marathi musical Sangeet Sharada was staged in his honour.

A Nashik group headed by Krishnaji Karve had resolved to eliminate Jackson early in 1910, but when Jackson was promoted to Commissioner of Bombay at the end of 1909, Karve, Vinayak Deshpande and Kanhere decided to strike before his transfer. Kanhere volunteered to fire the shots and carried poison to take his own life if caught, so as to shield his comrades; Deshpande and Karve also carried weapons as backup, in case his attempt failed.

On 21 December 1909, as the play drew toward its close, Kanhere rose from the audience and fired four shots into Jackson, killing him. He had carried poison to take his own life and avoid capture, but the crowd seized him before he could use it. The backup plan, in which Vinayak Deshpande and Karve also carried weapons, was never needed.

Kanhere was tried by the Bombay court and hanged at Thane prison on 19 April 1910, barely four months after the shooting. Krishnaji Karve, aged twenty-three, and Vinayak Deshpande, aged twenty-one, were hanged with him. The murder created a sensation in Nashik, Pune and Bombay, and led to the sweeping Nashik Conspiracy Case in which dozens of Abhinav Bharat members were prosecuted. Kanhere's youth, he was barely out of school, made him an enduring symbol of how deeply the revolutionary impulse had reached into the younger generation.
