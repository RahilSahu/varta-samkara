Vasudeo Hari Chapekar (1880 - 8 May 1899), also spelt Wasudev, was the youngest of the three Chapekar brothers of Poona, barely eighteen when he joined his brothers in the 1897 assassination of the British plague commissioner. Born into the modest Chapekar household at Chinchwad, where his father Haripant raised the boys on devotional kirtans and traditional values, Vasudeo grew up alongside Damodar and Balkrishna in the shadow of the same outrage that radicalised them.

That outrage was the bubonic plague of 1896-97 and the colonial state's answer to it. A Special Plague Committee under W. C. Rand, an Indian Civil Service officer, brought more than eight hundred soldiers into Poona to enforce measures that included forced entry into private homes, the stripping and examination of occupants including women in public, evacuation to hospitals and segregation camps, and the vandalism of property and religious symbols. The citizens' complaints were ignored, and the brothers resolved that Rand must die.

Like his brothers, Vasudeo had trained with the Chapekar Club, the youth body of some six hundred members the brothers founded after their meeting with Tilak, drilling in sword fighting, pistol firing and stick fighting on the open ground behind Parvati hill.

On the night of 22 June 1897, as Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee celebrations wound down at Government House, the brothers ambushed Rand's carriage on a dark road. Damodar shot Rand at point-blank range and Balkrishna killed his military escort, Lieutenant Ayerst. Vasudeo, the youngest conspirator, stood with them in the plot, and shared in the warning letter the brothers had sent Rand beforehand, declaring he would be killed that night rather than struck down unawares.

What followed tested him beyond the assassination itself. Damodar was arrested in October 1897 and hanged on 18 April 1898. Undeterred, Vasudeo sought vengeance against those who had betrayed his brothers to the British, deepening his entanglement with the colonial police. He was eventually captured, tried, and hanged on 8 May 1899, four days before Balkrishna, who had been betrayed and caught that January, followed him to the gallows on 12 May.

The three Chapekar brothers are described as the only instance anywhere in the world of three brothers hanged for their country's independence. Vasudeo, the teenager among them, is the least remembered of the three, his name surviving mainly in the collective phrase "the Chapekar brothers." Their ancestral home at Chinchwad, the Chapekar Wada, has been rebuilt as a memorial, but the youngest brother's individual story has largely faded from public memory.
