Saraswathi Rajamani: Teenage intelligence operative in the Indian National Army, 1943-1945

Saraswathi Rajamani was born on 11 January 1927 in Rangoon, Burma. Her father owned a gold mine and was among the richest Indians in the city, and her family were staunch supporters of the freedom movement, financing it with their wealth. She grew up in a household where the independence struggle was a living presence.

Her life changed in 1943 when, at sixteen, she heard Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose speak in Rangoon. Moved by his call, she donated all her gold and diamond jewellery to the Indian National Army. Bose visited her home to return the jewellery, suspecting the donation was impulsive, but she refused to take it back. Impressed by her resolve, he gave her the name Saraswathi. The same year she joined the Rani of Jhansi Regiment and was recruited into the INA's military intelligence wing, becoming one of the youngest operatives in the force.

Disguised as a boy under the alias "Mani", she worked as a servant inside British camps, intercepting orders and intelligence for the INA for nearly two years. According to several accounts, she was sent as a worker into a British military base in Kolkata and played a part in uncovering a British plan to assassinate Bose during his secret visit to the Indian border in 1943. Her most daring act came when a fellow spy was captured: disguised as a dancer, she infiltrated the British camp, drugged the officers in charge, and freed her colleague. While escaping, a British guard shot her in the leg. She got away, but the wound left her with a permanent limp.

For her service she was awarded a medal by the Japanese emperor and held the rank of Lieutenant in the Rani of Jhansi Brigade. When the INA disbanded after the Second World War, her family gave away their wealth, including the gold mine, and returned to India. She settled in Chennai, where customs officials confiscated her belongings, including Bose's letter of appreciation and the Japanese medal. She lived in poverty for years. After the media highlighted her condition, then Tamil Nadu chief minister Jayalalithaa gave her a one-bedroom apartment and financial aid; during the 2004 tsunami, Rajamani donated the aid money to the homeless. She also donated her insignia to the INA gallery of the Netaji Subhash Birthplace National Museum in Cuttack.

She died in Chennai on 13 January 2018, aged 91. Her story was revived by the 2016 short film "Voice of an Independent Indian" and by the Hindi and English e-book "Saraswathi Rajamani: The Forgotten Spy". Despite this, she remains one of the least known of the INA's operatives, a teenager who spied inside British camps and walked out with their secrets, and then spent the rest of her life quietly stitching clothes for orphaned children.
