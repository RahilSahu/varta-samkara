---
title: "Haipou Jadonang: The Naga Messiah-King Hanged in 1931"
date: 2026-10-02
source: "Wikipedia, Assam Times, Testbook"
---

Jadonang Malangmei, known as Haipou Jadonang, was a Rongmei Naga spiritual leader and political activist who founded the Heraka movement and was hanged by the British in 1931 for defying colonial rule. He was born on 10 June 1905 at Puiluan, also called Kambiron, village in the Tamenglong district of Manipur, the youngest of the three sons of Thiudai and Tabonliu, of the Malangmei clan. His father died when he was about a year old, and his mother raised the three boys by farming the family land.

In the 1920s Jadonang founded the Heraka movement, a socio-religious revival built on the ancestral Naga religion. He preached the ancient faith established by the Naga forefathers, sought to reform society on the foundations of customary law, and declared himself the "messiah king" of the Nagas. He saw the growing influence of Christianity in the Naga hills as a sign of foreign imperialism and a threat to the traditional religion and society of his people. His movement spread widely through the Zeliangrong territory, the Naga-inhabited region at the junction of Assam, Manipur, and Nagaland, in the years before the large-scale conversion to Christianity.

The movement soon became political. Jadonang envisioned an independent Naga kingdom, the Makam Gwangdi or Naga Raj, and that brought him into direct conflict with the British, who then controlled the Naga territories completely. He denounced the forced porter system, the heavy hill house tax of three rupees a year, and the imposition of new colonial laws, and he organized an armed wing called the Riphen to protect his people. He had heard of Mahatma Gandhi's Civil Disobedience movement and was influenced by it, though his own path was his own. He went into prolonged trances, sometimes lasting days, did penance at the Bhuban Cave near Silchar, and returned, in the accounts of his followers, with divine power and enlightenment from Lord Tingwang.

The British could not tolerate a leader with that kind of hold over the Zeliangrong. Jadonang was arrested, tried, and on 13 June 1931 declared guilty of murders. His supporters have always maintained that he was implicated in a false murder case to get rid of him. He was hanged on 29 August 1931 on the bank of the Nambul river, behind the Imphal jail. He was twenty-six.

The British also demolished the mandir he had established at Kambiron, trying to uproot the movement with its founder. It did not work. His cousin, the sixteen-year-old Gaidinliu, took up the Heraka cause and carried it through fourteen years of British imprisonment. Jadonang Martyr's Day is observed every 29 August in Manipur, but outside the Northeast his name is almost unknown, another casualty of a freedom struggle whose history was written far from the hills where he lived and died.
