Neera Arya: The INA's first woman spy, who killed her husband to save Netaji

Neera Arya was born on 5 March 1902 in Khekra Nagar, in the Baghpat district of Uttar Pradesh, into an affluent Arya Samaj family. Her father, the businessman Seth Chhajjumal, made sure she and her brother Basant were educated in Kolkata. From an early age she was drawn to the cause of Indian independence, and in 1942 she joined the Rani Jhansi Regiment of the Azad Hind Fauj, the Indian National Army led by Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose.

Her father had arranged her marriage to Shrikant Jai Ranjan Das, a British Army officer posted as a CID inspector. The marriage foundered on ideology: Neera was devoted to the freedom movement, while her husband served the British government. When Shrikant discovered her links with Bose's forces, he pressed her to reveal Bose's whereabouts. One day he followed her to a clandestine meeting and fired at Bose. The shot struck Bose's driver. Accounts differ on whether the driver was killed or only wounded; what the sources agree on is that Neera then shot her husband dead, choosing the nation and her leader over her marriage.

Impressed by her loyalty and nerve, Bose appointed her the INA's first woman spy. Working alongside operatives like Saraswathi Rajamani and disguised as a man, she gathered intelligence from British officers' homes and military camps, and she also helped smuggle weapons and supplies to the army.

The British arrested her in 1943. She was charged with the murder of a British CID officer, sedition, and espionage, and sentenced to life imprisonment in the Cellular Jail in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. There she was offered her freedom in exchange for information about Bose and the Congress leadership. She refused, and when a guard mocked her she spat at him. Historical accounts record that she was then subjected to a brutal mutilation of her breasts as punishment for her silence. She never betrayed the cause.

Neera Arya died on 26 July 1998 in Hyderabad. After independence she lived in obscurity, far from the recognition her sacrifice merited. She wrote an autobiography, "Mera Jeevan, Mera Sangharsh" (My Life, My Struggle), and was known in her later years as "Neera Nagini" and "Peddamma" (elder mother). A statue of her stands in Hyderabad, and she is remembered at the Cellular Jail memorial. The Government of India's Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav lists her among the country's unsung heroes, and filmmaker Roopa Iyer announced a biopic on her life. Her story is one of extraordinary personal sacrifice followed by decades of near-total public silence.
