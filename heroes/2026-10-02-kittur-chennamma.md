Kittur Rani Chennamma (23 October 1778, 21 February 1829) led an armed rebellion against the British East India Company in 1824, thirty-three years before the uprising of 1857, and she won the first battle. She is remembered as the first Indian queen to defeat the British on the battlefield, and she paid for it with the rest of her life in prison.

She was born in Kakati, in present-day Belagavi district of Karnataka, into a Lingayat family, and was trained from childhood in horse riding, sword fighting and archery. She married Raja Mallasarja of the princely state of Kittur. After his death in 1816 his son Shivalingarudra Sarja took the throne, and when he died in 1824 he had adopted a boy, Shivalingappa, as his heir. The British, under collector John Thackeray of Dharwad, refused to recognise the adoption and ordered the annexation of Kittur under the Doctrine of Lapse.

Chennamma refused to surrender her kingdom. In October 1824 the British attacked with a force of some twenty thousand men and four hundred guns. Her army, commanded in part by her lieutenant Sangolli Rayanna, inflicted heavy losses: Thackeray himself was killed, reportedly by her lieutenant Amatur Balappa, and two British officers, Walter Elliot and Stevenson, were taken hostage. Chennamma released the hostages after the British promised the war would end. The promise was broken. The British returned under Chaplin with reinforcements from Mysore and Sholapur. In the second battle another British officer, Munro, the nephew of Sir Thomas Munro and sub-collector of Solapur, was killed, but Chennamma's forces were outnumbered and, by some accounts, betrayed from within. She was captured along with Rayanna and her lieutenant Gurusiddappa.

She was imprisoned for life in Bailhongal Fort, where her health broke down. She died there on 21 February 1829, and her samadhi stands at Bailhongal. Her adopted son's cause did not die with her: Sangolli Rayanna escaped, raised a peasant guerrilla army, and fought the British for two more years before his capture and hanging in 1831.

Kittur celebrates her every year in the Kittur Utsav, held from 22 to 24 October to mark her first victory. Her story reached the screen in B. R. Panthulu's 1961 film "Kitturu Chennamma," starring B. Saroja Devi as the queen and Dr. Rajkumar as Raja Mallasarja, which won the best feature film award at the 9th National Film Awards. The Rani Chennamma Express still runs daily between Bengaluru and Miraj. Outside Karnataka, though, the revolt of 1857 is treated as the beginning of armed resistance to the British, and Chennamma's war, fought a generation earlier and won on the field, is left out of the telling. The correction is simple: the first queen to beat the Company in battle was not in 1857. She was in Kittur, in 1824.
