---
title: "Alluri Sitarama Raju: The Manyam Veerudu of the Rampa Rebellion"
date: 2026-10-02
source: "News18, GKToday"
---

For two years the British Empire hunted one man through the forests of the Eastern Ghats, spending a fortune and deploying special forces, and when they caught him they tied him to a tree and shot him. He was twenty-six. His name was Alluri Sitarama Raju, called Manyam Veerudu, the Hero of the Jungle.

Raju was born on 4 July 1897, in Pandrangi in the Visakhapatnam region of the Madras Presidency (some accounts give 1898 as the year, and others name Mogallu in West Godavari as his birthplace). Born Alluri Rama Raju, he later prefixed "Sita" to his name. His father Venkata Rama Raju was a photographer who died when Raju was young; his mother Suryanarayanamma raised him. He studied at Bhimavaram, Narsapuram and Visakhapatnam, took up sannyasa at eighteen, and drifted into the tribal belt of the Agency tracts, living among the Koya and Konda Reddi peoples.

The tribals' grievances were the making of the revolt. The Madras Forest Act of 1882 banned podu, their traditional shifting cultivation, barred them from forest lands, and enforced vetti, unpaid forced labour. A British officer named Bastian extracted vetti ruthlessly for road construction in the Gudem hills. Inspired by Gandhi's non-cooperation movement, Raju first urged the tribals to wear khadi and give up alcohol; then he concluded the forest laws could only be broken by force.

The Rampa Rebellion ran from August 1922 to May 1924. It opened on 22 August 1922 with a raid on the Chintapalle police station, followed by strikes on Krishnadevipeta, Rajavommangi, Addateegala, Rampachodavaram and Devipatnam. Raju's men seized guns and ammunition in daylight and, in a gesture that became legend, left signed notes in each raided station listing exactly what they had taken and daring the police to stop them. His commanders, Gam Mallu Dora and Gantam Dora, led a force the British could not corner in the hills.

The Empire answered with the Assam Rifles and the Malabar Special Police under Special Commissioner Rutherford, in a manhunt that lasted nearly two years and, by some accounts, cost over forty lakh rupees of that era's money. In May 1924 Raju was trapped at Koyyuru village in the Chintapalle forests, captured, tied to a tree, and shot by a firing squad on 7 May 1924 at Mampa village. A mausoleum holding his remains stands at Krishnadevipeta.

Raju is not unknown in Andhra Pradesh: India Post issued a stamp in his honour in 1986, a district was named Alluri Sitarama Raju, and the Prime Minister unveiled his statue in Bhimavaram in 2022. Nationally, though, he is a film character, fictionalized in RRR, rather than a studied figure. He left no writings, no party, and no political heirs, only a two-year war in the forests and the memory of a young man who signed his name to every raid he led.
