Khan Bahadur Khan: The Rohilkhand viceroy who held Bareilly for a year

Khan Bahadur Khan (d. 1860) was the grandson of Hafiz Rahmat Khan, the Nawab of Rohilkhand, who formed his own government in Bareilly during the 1857 uprising and held the region against the British for nearly a year. An elderly pensioner and retired subedar of the Company's army, the Indian Express describes him as 82 in 1858, though some reference works give his birth as 1823, so the record of his age is uncertain. When the Bareilly sepoys rose on 31 May 1857, Bakht Khan was made their general before marching to Delhi; by unanimous agreement Khan Bahadur Khan was then appointed the leader of Rohilkhand, and Bahadur Shah Zafar's government named him its viceroy in the region.

He governed with an eight-member committee of Hindus and Muslims, with Khushi Ram as his deputy, Shoba Ram as diwan, and Niyaz Muhammad commanding his troops. In a deliberate gesture of communal unity he banned cow slaughter to respect Hindu sentiment, and Hindus and Muslims fought shoulder to shoulder under his leadership. For almost a year Bareilly functioned as an independent principality, one of the last places in northern India to be retaken by the British, falling nearly ten months after the siege of Delhi. His grave lies in the old district jail at Bareilly. His forces defeated the British in several engagements.

On the morning of 5 May 1858 he led a garrison estimated at over 35,000 men out of Bareilly to face Sir Colin Campbell's forces at the Nukutte Bridge. Campbell's biographer Adrian Greenwood records that at 7 am the British marched in two lines, and as they approached the bridge the rebels opened fire. Professor Iqbal Husain writes that Khan Bahadur Khan remained hopeful "till very late that Bareilly would not fall to the Company as long as Lucknow remained free." After a hard fight the better-armed British prevailed, and the city fell.

He fled toward Nepal, but the Nepalese captured him and handed him over to the British. Tried and sentenced to death, he was hanged at the old kotwali in Bareilly on 24 February 1860, though some accounts give March 1860. His deputy Khushi Ram was hanged alongside him. His grave lies in the old district jail at Bareilly. A ruler who kept Rohilkhand free for a year after Delhi fell, he is absent from most national accounts of 1857, a regional leader eclipsed by the bigger names of the revolt.
