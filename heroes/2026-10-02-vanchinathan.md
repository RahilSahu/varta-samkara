Vanchinathan, popularly known as Vanchi, was born Shankaran in 1886 at Sengottai, then in Travancore and now in Tenkasi district of Tamil Nadu, to Ragupathy Iyer and Rukmani. A graduate from a poor Tamil Brahmin family, he began his working life as a temple accountant at Ariankavu in January 1908 and was soon appointed a forest ranger at Punalur. He married Ponnamma; their daughter died in infancy.

His radicalisation was methodical. In April 1910, Neelakanta Brahmachari formed a branch of the Bharatmatha Association at Punalur, and Vanchinathan joined it. He absorbed the revolutionary literature coming out of Pondicherry, visited the town to meet V.V.S. Iyer, and in 1911 learned to shoot under his guidance. The extremist circle around him had decided that Robert William d'Escourt Ashe, the acting Collector and District Magistrate of Tirunelveli, had to be eliminated. Ashe had suppressed the Swadeshi movement led by V.O. Chidambaram Pillai and others in Tirunelveli and Tuticorin, quelled the Tinnevelly protests of 1908 with brutality, and played a role in the collapse of Chidambaram's Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company.

On 17 June 1911, Ashe and his wife left Tirunelveli Junction by the 9.30 a.m. train to board the Boat Mail at Maniyachi Junction. As the couple waited, Vanchinathan, dressed in a green coat and white dhoti, entered the first-class compartment and shot Ashe point blank in the chest with a pistol. According to British police documents, the weapon was a Belgian-made Browning automatic sent by Madam Cama, the revolutionary in Paris, from the Abhinav Bharat network. After the shooting, Vanchinathan ran along the platform, took cover in the station master's room, and shot himself in the mouth. He was found dead some time later. A youth named Sankara Krishna Aiyar, who had accompanied him, fled but was later caught and convicted. Fourteen persons were charged in the case that became known as the Tirunelveli conspiracy case.

Ashe was the first and only colonial official to be assassinated in South India during the independence movement, and the killing rattled the Raj. The Maniyachi railway station was renamed Vanchi Maniyachi in 1988, and the Tamil Nadu government built a memorial hall for Vanchinathan at Sengottai in 2013.

Vanchinathan was 24 or 25 when he died. Unlike Bhagat Singh, he left no jail diary and no courtroom speeches, only a brief, violent act and the questions it raised about the price of revolutionary terror. His memory in Tamil Nadu is honoured, and also contested, with some Dalit and Dravidian groups marking Ashe's death anniversary in his favour. But the bare facts remain: a young forest ranger who believed the Company's man in Tirunelveli had to die, and who died with him.
