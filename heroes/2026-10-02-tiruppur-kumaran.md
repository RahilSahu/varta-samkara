Kumaraswamy Mudaliyar, known to history as Tiruppur Kumaran, was born on 4 October 1904 at Chennimalai in what is now Erode district, Tamil Nadu. His parents were handloom weavers, too poor to send him to school. On 19 March 1925, at the house of the freedom fighter Padmavathy Asher, he met Mahatma Gandhi for the first time, and from that day he became an ardent follower of Gandhi and of satyagraha. He founded the Desa Bandhu Youth Association, which organised anti-British protest marches across Tamil Nadu, picketed shops selling foreign cloth and liquor, and promoted khadi and village industries.

When Gandhi launched the Dandi March in 1930, Kumaran wanted to join the Vedaranyam procession in the Madras presidency led by C. Rajagopalachari. His parents opposed it, and in a mark of solidarity he went on a hunger strike for a whole day in Tiruppur. It was the act of a young man who had no wealth, no title, and no platform, only conviction.

The decisive day came on 10 January 1932. Gandhi had been arrested on 4 January at Yeravada and the Civil Disobedience Movement had resumed; public processions were outlawed and the national tricolour was banned. The Desabandhu Youth Association, led by Kumaran, took out a peaceful procession from Mangala Vilas, a freedom fighter's residence, along the banks of the Noyyal river in Tiruppur, carrying the Swaraj flag. When the nine-member procession crossed the police station, the police launched a lathi charge and brutally assaulted the marchers.

Kumaran was struck repeatedly and suffered severe head injuries, but he refused to let the flag fall or touch the ground, clutching it to his chest even as he fell unconscious. He was taken to the Tiruppur General Hospital, where he died on 11 January 1932, at the age of 27. He became known across Tamil Nadu as Kodi Kaatha Kumaran, the one who saved the flag.

His martyrdom sent a wave of anger through the Madras Presidency and showed how the freedom struggle had moved beyond urban elites to handloom weavers, mill workers, and rural youth. A memorial to him was built on Kumaran Salai near the railway station in Tiruppur, and his epithet Kodi Kaatha Kumaran is still invoked in Tamil Nadu's political memory, including in official tributes on his birth anniversary each October. He played no part in the negotiations that eventually freed India, but his death, holding a banned flag upright, remains one of the starkest images of what the movement cost ordinary young Indians.
