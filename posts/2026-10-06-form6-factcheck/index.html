CJI Says Supreme Court Did Not Approve Any Form 6 Modifications; ECI, PIB Issue Fact-Checks

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant said on Monday that the Supreme Court had not approved "any" of the modifications made to Form 6, the form voters use to register in the electoral roll. The remark came as a three-judge bench headed by the CJI heard petitions filed against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over changes to the voter registration process.

The observation landed amid a political slugfest over the Form 6 changes, which followed reports of dissent within the Election Commission over the revisions. Opposition parties have alleged that the changes were made to alter electoral rolls, while the EC has defended the process as routine administrative updating.

ECI and PIB respond with fact-checks

Following the CJI's remarks, the Election Commission of India and the Press Information Bureau put out fact-checks addressing claims about Form 6. The EC's position had been set out in a press release on September 26, in which it claimed that the declaration form attached with Form 6 was upheld by the apex court.

That claim is now at the centre of the controversy: the CJI's statement directly contradicts the commission's reading of what the court approved. The court also issued notices to the government and the Election Commission seeking responses within a week on a separate PIL challenging decisions allegedly taken by the EC without the approval of two Election Commissioners.

Reactions across parties

The BJP said the Supreme Court's decision to issue notices to the EC and the Centre demonstrated the independence of institutions and showed that democratic processes were functioning, countering opposition claims of an atmosphere of fear. Opposition leaders, meanwhile, continued to press for the CEC's removal, with former bureaucrats and civil society activists submitting a "chargesheet" accusing Gyanesh Kumar of six instances of proved misbehaviour.

Separately, the INDIA bloc has announced an Aakrosh March in Bihar on October 7 against the CEC, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Amit Shah, followed by district-level sit-ins on October 8 over alleged irregularities in the special intensive revision of electoral rolls.
