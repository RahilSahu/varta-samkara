Nobel Prize in Medicine 2026 Goes to Trio Behind Optogenetics

On this page
  • The discovery
  • What the committee said
  • Nobel week continues

American scientist Karl Deisseroth and German researchers Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel have won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for "discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics", the Nobel Assembly at Sweden's Karolinska Institutet announced on October 5. The three will share a prize sum of 12 million Swedish crowns, about 1.2 million US dollars.

Optogenetics is a technique that lets scientists switch the activity of individual nerve cells on or off using light, giving researchers a way to map which brain cells control which functions.

The discovery

In the 1990s, Hegemann, then at the Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry in Germany, investigated how a single-celled alga swims toward light and found a light-sensitive protein. Working with Nagel in the 2000s, he showed that the protein, called channelrhodopsin, opens a channel that lets ions flow when exposed to light.

Deisseroth, at Stanford University in the United States, took the work further, showing that the protein could be used to control nerve cells. In work published in 2005, he genetically engineered nerve cells from rats to produce channelrhodopsin so they could be activated by light, and later showed the system works inside the brains of living mice.

What the committee said

Thomas Perlmann, secretary-general of the Nobel Assembly, said the method makes it possible to switch on or off the activity of individual nerve cells in a living brain, and that it is now used in laboratories around the world to reveal the brain's mysteries.

Committee member Anna Wedell said the work laid the foundation for a new era in neuroscience, adding that for the first time researchers can start to understand how the brain processes information and how different neurons interact. The technique is also being explored for possible treatments of vision disorders and neurological and psychiatric conditions.

Nobel week continues

The medicine prize traditionally opens the annual Nobel announcements. Prizes in physics, chemistry, literature, peace and economics will follow over the coming days, with the peace prize set to be announced on October 9.
