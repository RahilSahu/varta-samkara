India invokes emergency powers to run coal plants at full throttle

The power ministry has invoked emergency powers to run 112 captive coal plants at maximum capacity from October 1 to December 31, 2026, citing critically low coal stocks and rising electricity demand.

The order, issued under Section 11 of the Electricity Act, covers all captive plants of 50 MW and above. Nearly 40 percent of coal-fired plants are running critically low on coal, while hotter-than-usual El Nino weather is pushing electricity demand higher than normal for this time of year.

Plants covered and the conditions

The directive covers plants owned by some of India's largest industrial groups, including Tata Steel, Vedanta, Hindalco, JSW Steel, UltraTech Cement, Reliance, Indian Oil and Nayara Energy. Any surplus power generated must be sold on power exchanges rather than hoarded, and the plants are required to file weekly reports with the Central Electricity Authority.

In a separate but related move, Tata Power's Mundra imported-coal plant has also been told to run at full capacity through December 31.

Section 11 is a rarely used provision that allows the government to direct generating stations to operate in the public interest during extraordinary circumstances. Its invocation signals that the power ministry expects a tight supply situation through the winter months, and wants every available megawatt on the grid.
