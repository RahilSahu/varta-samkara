India just beamed unhackable quantum keys through open air: 5.56 km, a national first.

What we know:

- QNu Labs, working with BISAG-N and IIT Gandhinagar, demonstrated India's first free-space Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) link over 5.56 km. The field trial ran on the night of September 27-28, and the Ministry of Electronics and IT announced it on October 3. - The secure channel linked BISAG-N and IIT Gandhinagar using QNu Labs' Pointing, Acquisition and Tracking system. It held a Quantum Bit Error Rate below 5% and generated secure keys at 230-260 bits per second. - The keys were fed into BISAG-N's post-quantum cryptography-enabled Vedic Kavach platform, and test messages were encrypted and decrypted end to end. - The system pairs two layers of protection: QNu Labs' hardware QKD device Armos over the free-space optical channel, and Vedic Kavach's software-based post-quantum cryptography with quantum random number generation. The integrated design keeps security intact even if the physical quantum channel drops temporarily. - Why it matters: free-space QKD sends cryptographic keys through open air instead of relying on fiber alone, a step toward longer-distance and satellite-based quantum networks that can withstand quantum-era threats.
