Trump Says US Will Not Attack Iran Before November 3 Midterms as Pentagon Prepares Strike Options

President Donald Trump said on Thursday that the United States will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm congressional elections, even as the Pentagon, acting on his orders, develops options for an intensive three-day military campaign against Iranian targets.

In a social media post, Trump said the US was having "productive discussions" with the Islamic Republic of Iran and declared the country would not be allowed a nuclear weapon, while confirming that a US-led blockade on the Strait of Hormuz would remain in full force. The New York Times reported that senior national security officials, led by Vice President JD Vance, discussed strike proposals at Camp David last Friday, centred on a short, intense campaign targeting Iran's missile and drone arsenal, energy facilities and Revolutionary Guard command posts. The plans were first reported by Axios.

No final decision to launch strikes has been made, officials said. The Times also reported that Trump has repeatedly rejected proposals to resume major combat operations, turning down five such plans in recent months, but has now asked for military options again, leaving open renewed strikes after the elections. The build-up coincides with the deployment of three US carrier strike groups in the Middle East, the first such concentration since 2003. Indirect negotiations between Washington and Tehran continue, though disagreements over Iran's nuclear programme remain.
