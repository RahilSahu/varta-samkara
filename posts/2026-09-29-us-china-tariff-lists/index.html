US AND CHINA RELEASE $30B TARIFF-CUT LISTS

Days after the Trump-Xi summit in Washington, the US and China released reciprocal lists of products that will get tariff cuts, worth about $30 billion each way.

The lists cover 77 Chinese goods, including fireworks, Christmas ornaments, soccer balls, toys, microwave ovens and fish hooks, and 1,619 US products, including poultry, dairy, peanuts, noodles, silk, breeding horses, timber, medical equipment, infant formula and coal.

Over 90% of the listed products will drop to standard most-favored-nation tariff levels, effectively wiping out country-specific tariffs on them. The wider trade truce, which was set to expire on November 10, has been extended to January.

USTR Jamieson Greer says the deal improves market access for about 30% of US exports to China. But chips, EVs and batteries are not covered, and analysts caution the overall impact may be limited. Two-way trade was $495 billion in 2025, down 25% year on year.

What comes next

Next up on the calendar: APEC in Shenzhen in November and the G20 in Miami in December, where the two sides are expected to keep negotiating. The lists are a confidence-building step, but the core technology disputes remain untouched.
