ANALYSIS | The so-called "breast tax" story is being deliberately misrepresented.

Mulakkaram was not a tax for covering or uncovering breasts. It was a poll tax on lower castes, named by body parts only to distinguish gender, just like Talakkaram (head tax) and Meeshakkaram (moustache tax). No historical record shows women were forced to stay topless because of this tax.

Multiple foreign travelers like Marco Polo, Pietro Della Valle, John Grose and James Forbes clearly record that women across Kerala, including royalty, did not cover their upper bodies as a cultural norm. Even queens like Umayamma of Attingal were topless in durbar. This had nothing to do with caste humiliation or Brahminism.

The tax itself was an oppressive revenue policy of the Travancore state during wartime and resource shortage, and it was abolished by 1864-65. The extreme mutilation story comes from folklore recorded by L.A. Krishna Iyer and refers to Arayans, not Ezhavas. It reflects the cruelty of tax collectors, not religious doctrine.

As for Nangeli, there is zero historical record before a 2016 BBC article based on an artist's paintings. That is modern mythology, not history.
