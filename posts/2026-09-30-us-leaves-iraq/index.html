After 23 years, America leaves Iraq

US forces are withdrawing from their remaining bases in Iraq today, ending more than two decades of American military presence.

The pullout was agreed in 2024 under Joe Biden and is being carried out by Donald Trump's administration, even as Washington wages war against Iran.

Tehran is celebrating the exit as a victory of its "axis of resistance". But analysts warn of a security vacuum: Iran-backed militias could expand their power, and ISIS sleeper cells have shown movement in recent weeks.

23 years, summed up

2003: invasion topples Saddam. 2011: US pulls out. 2014: US returns to help crush ISIS. 2026: the final exit. 4,500 American service members died in Iraq.

The withdrawal closes the longest-running chapter of America's post-9/11 wars, but the warnings about what fills the vacuum are as loud as the celebrations.
