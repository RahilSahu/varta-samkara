Key Articles of the Indian Constitution: Simple Summaries for Students

11 min read

On this page

The Constitution of India is organised into Articles. An Article is a single numbered provision of the Constitution, and the Articles are grouped into Parts by subject. Almost every competitive exam, from UPSC and judiciary exams to SSC and state PCS, asks questions that begin with "Which Article deals with...". This page explains the most frequently asked Articles in simple language, one by one.

Fundamental Rights (Articles 12 to 35)

Article 12: It defines the term "State" for the purpose of Fundamental Rights, covering the government, Parliament, state legislatures, and other authorities.

Article 13: It declares that any law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights is void, and it is the basis of judicial review in India.

Article 14: It guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of the laws to every person in India.

Article 15: It prohibits discrimination against any citizen on the grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.

Article 16: It guarantees equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters of public employment.

Article 17: It abolishes untouchability and makes its practice in any form an offence punishable by law.

Article 18: It abolishes titles, so the state cannot confer titles except military and academic distinctions.

Article 19: It protects six freedoms: speech and expression, peaceful assembly, association, free movement, residence and settlement, and practising any profession or occupation. These freedoms are subject to reasonable restrictions.

Article 20: It protects persons accused of offences: no punishment under a law made after the act (ex post facto law), no punishment twice for the same offence (double jeopardy), and no person can be forced to be a witness against himself.

Article 21: It guarantees that no person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Courts have read many implied rights into it, including the right to privacy and a clean environment.

Article 21A: Added by the 86th Amendment in 2002, it makes free and compulsory education a fundamental right for children between 6 and 14 years.

Article 22: It provides safeguards to persons who are arrested or detained, including the right to be informed of the grounds of arrest and the right to consult a lawyer.

Article 23: It prohibits traffic in human beings, begar, and other forms of forced labour.

Article 24: It prohibits the employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines, or other hazardous work.

Article 25: It guarantees freedom of conscience and the right to freely profess, practise, and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality, and health.

Article 26: It gives every religious denomination the right to manage its own religious affairs, own property, and establish institutions.

Article 27: It says no person can be compelled to pay taxes for the promotion or maintenance of any particular religion.

Article 28: It prohibits religious instruction in educational institutions wholly maintained by state funds.

Article 29: It protects the right of minorities to conserve their distinct language, script, and culture.

Article 30: It gives minorities the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.

Article 32: It gives every person the right to move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Dr. Ambedkar called it the heart and soul of the Constitution. (Article 31, which dealt with the right to property, was repealed by the 44th Amendment in 1978.)

Articles 33, 34 and 35: Article 33 lets Parliament restrict Fundamental Rights of armed forces and police personnel. Article 34 allows restrictions during martial law. Article 35 gives Parliament, not state legislatures, the power to make laws giving effect to Fundamental Rights.

Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 36 to 51)

The Directive Principles are guidelines for the state in making laws. They are not enforceable in courts, but governments are expected to apply them while governing.

Article 38: It directs the state to secure a social order that promotes the welfare of the people by minimising inequalities in income, status, and opportunities.

Article 39: It lays down principles for both men and women equally, including an adequate means of livelihood, fair distribution of resources, equal pay for equal work, and protection of workers and children from exploitation.

Article 39A: It directs the state to provide free legal aid so that justice is not denied for economic reasons.

Article 40: It directs the state to organise village panchayats and give them powers to function as units of self-government.

Article 41: It directs the state to secure the right to work, to education, and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, and sickness, within its economic capacity.

Article 42: It directs the state to secure just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.

Article 43: It directs the state to secure a living wage and decent conditions of work for all workers.

Article 44: It directs the state to endeavour to secure a uniform civil code for all citizens throughout India.

Article 45: It directs the state to provide early childhood care and education for all children below six years of age.

Article 46: It directs the state to promote the educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and other weaker sections.

Article 47: It directs the state to raise nutrition levels and improve public health, and to prohibit intoxicating drinks and drugs harmful to health.

Article 48: It directs the state to organise agriculture and animal husbandry, and to prohibit the slaughter of cows, calves, and other milch and draught cattle.

Article 48A: It directs the state to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.

Article 49: It directs the state to protect monuments and places of artistic or historic interest from damage and destruction.

Article 50: It directs the state to separate the judiciary from the executive in public services.

Article 51: It directs the state to promote international peace and security and maintain just relations between nations.

The Union Executive and Parliament

Article 52: It provides that there shall be a President of India.

Article 53: It vests the executive power of the Union in the President, to be exercised directly or through officers subordinate to him.

Article 54: It provides for the election of the President by an electoral college of elected MPs and MLAs.

Article 55: It lays down the manner of the President's election, giving weightage to votes based on population.

Article 61: It lays down the procedure for the impeachment of the President for violation of the Constitution.

Article 72: It gives the President the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, and remissions of punishment, including in death sentence cases.

Article 74: It provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President.

Article 75: It provides that the Prime Minister is appointed by the President, other ministers on the PM's advice, and that the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

Article 76: It provides for the Attorney-General of India, the chief legal adviser to the government, appointed by the President.

Article 79: It provides that Parliament consists of the President, the Council of States (Rajya Sabha), and the House of the People (Lok Sabha).

Article 80: It deals with the composition of the Rajya Sabha: not more than 250 members, of whom 238 represent states and union territories and 12 are nominated by the President for their contribution to art, literature, science, and social service.

Article 81: It deals with the composition of the Lok Sabha, with a maximum strength of 552 members.

Article 83: It fixes the duration of the Houses of Parliament: the Lok Sabha normally lasts five years, while the Rajya Sabha is a continuing body with one-third of members retiring every two years.

Article 85: It gives the President the power to summon and prorogue Parliament and to dissolve the Lok Sabha.

Article 108: It provides for a joint sitting of both Houses, presided over by the Speaker, to resolve deadlocks on ordinary bills. It does not apply to money bills or constitutional amendment bills.

Article 110: It defines a money bill and declares that the Speaker's decision on whether a bill is a money bill is final.

Article 112: It requires the President to lay the Annual Financial Statement, commonly called the Union Budget, before Parliament every year.

Article 123: It gives the President the power to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session. An ordinance has the force of law but lapses if not approved within six weeks of Parliament reassembling.

Judiciary

Article 124: It provides for the establishment of the Supreme Court of India.

Article 129: It declares the Supreme Court a court of record, meaning its judgments are recorded and have evidentiary value.

Article 136: It gives the Supreme Court discretionary power to grant special leave to appeal against any judgment of any court or tribunal in India.

Article 141: It declares that the law laid down by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts in India.

Article 142: It empowers the Supreme Court to pass any order necessary for doing complete justice in a case before it.

Article 143: It gives the President the power to seek the Supreme Court's advisory opinion on questions of law or public importance.

Article 144: It requires all civil and judicial authorities in India to act in aid of the Supreme Court.

Article 214: It provides for a High Court for each state.

Article 226: It gives High Courts the power to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose, making their writ jurisdiction wider than the Supreme Court's under Article 32.

Centre-State Relations and Emergency

Article 245: It defines the territorial extent of laws made by Parliament and state legislatures.

Article 246: It distributes legislative subjects between the Union and the States through the Union List, State List, and Concurrent List in the Seventh Schedule.

Article 248: It gives Parliament exclusive power to legislate on residuary subjects, that is, subjects not listed in any of the three lists.

Article 249: It allows the Rajya Sabha, by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting, to authorise Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in the national interest.

Article 250: It gives Parliament the power to legislate on State List subjects while a national emergency is in operation.

Article 252: It allows Parliament to legislate for two or more states if those states pass resolutions requesting it.

Article 253: It gives Parliament the power to make laws for implementing international treaties and agreements.

Article 256: It obliges states to exercise their executive power so as to ensure compliance with Parliament's laws.

Article 257: It gives the Union control over states in certain cases, such as the construction and maintenance of means of communication of national importance.

Article 263: It allows the President to establish an Inter-State Council to coordinate between the Union and the states.

Article 267: It provides for the Contingency Fund of India, used for unforeseen expenditure with the President's approval.

Article 280: It provides for a Finance Commission every five years to recommend the distribution of tax revenues between the Union and the states.

Article 312: It allows the Rajya Sabha, by a two-thirds majority, to create new All-India Services such as the IAS and IPS.

Article 315: It provides for the Union Public Service Commission and State Public Service Commissions for recruitment to public services.

Article 324: It vests the superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the Election Commission of India.

Article 326: It provides that elections to the Lok Sabha and state assemblies are held on the basis of adult suffrage.

Article 343: It declares Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language of the Union.

Article 352: It provides for the proclamation of a national emergency on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion.

Article 356: It provides for President's Rule when the constitutional machinery in a state breaks down.

Article 360: It provides for a financial emergency if the financial stability of India is threatened. It has never been proclaimed.

Article 368: It lays down the procedure for amending the Constitution, through simple majority, special majority, or special majority with ratification by half the states.

Panchayats, Municipalities and Others

Article 243B: It provides for the constitution of panchayats at the village, intermediate, and district levels.

Article 243D: It reserves seats in panchayats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, and one-third of seats for women.

Article 243K: It provides for a State Election Commission to supervise the elections to panchayats.

Article 300A: It provides that no person shall be deprived of property except by authority of law. After the 44th Amendment in 1978, the right to property is a legal right, not a fundamental right.

Article 51A: It lists the Fundamental Duties of citizens. These are covered in detail in our Constitution summary for UPSC and law aspirants.

This is a study summary for exam preparation; for legal purposes, always consult the official text of the Constitution of India.
