Market outlook Bullish

Tactical upward bias with choppy consolidation. The Nifty posted its biggest weekly gain in 27 weeks (+2.79%), closed at the week's high of 23,146, FII selling is moderating, Q2 earnings from IT and banks are beating estimates, and valuations sit near long-run averages. This looks like a recovery phase, not a confirmed new bull run: expect stock-specific moves rather than a straight-line rally.

FII flows

FIIs turned net buyers on Friday 16 Oct (+Rs 1,166 crore, provisional NSE data), snapping a heavy selling streak, but remained net sellers for the 9th straight week. Weekly FII selling moderated sharply to Rs 8,991 crore from Rs 30,294 crore the previous week. FPIs have pulled about Rs 3.08 lakh crore out of Indian equities in 2026, nearly double the Rs 1.66 lakh crore of 2025.

DII flows

DIIs booked profits on Friday (-Rs 3,661 crore) but stayed net buyers for the 9th consecutive week at Rs 7,813 crore. Month-to-date, DII buying of Rs 48,168 crore has almost fully absorbed FII selling of Rs 48,769 crore, acting as the market's shock absorber.

What is driving the market
  • Q2FY27 earnings momentum: the Nifty IT index rose about 11.7% this week. Tech Mahindra jumped about 8% on its results, TCS gained 4.8%, Infosys 4.6%, Wipro about 5.7%. Banks reported strong Saturday results: ICICI Bank profit +20%, Axis Bank +38%, HDFC Bank +5%.
  • FII selling exhaustion: weekly FII outflows moderated to Rs 8,991 crore from Rs 30,294 crore the prior week, and FIIs turned net buyers (+Rs 1,166 crore) on Friday.
  • DII wall of money: month-to-date DII buying of Rs 48,168 crore has almost fully absorbed FII selling of Rs 48,769 crore.
  • Reasonable valuations: the Nifty trades at a PE of 19.77, close to its long-run average of about 20, and sits 12.2% below its 52-week high of 26,373.20, leaving recovery headroom.
  • Global relief: the late-September US-China '30-for-30' tariff framework (tariff cuts on about $60 billion of goods) and the extension of the trade truce to 10 Jan 2027 steadied risk sentiment; the US dollar index eased to 101.78.
  • Festive season demand: Dhanteras (6 Nov) and Diwali (8 Nov 2026) typically lift consumption, auto sales and gold buying.
Key risks
  • Brent crude near $106 per barrel keeps India's import bill and inflation risk elevated; USD/INR at 94.96 adds pressure.
  • FIIs are still net sellers for the 9th straight week. PL Capital cautions against expecting a V-shaped recovery and does not rule out a further dip before consolidation.
  • Weak 2026 monsoon: all-India rainfall was 87% of the long-period average, the worst since 2015, which could hurt rural demand and rabi sowing.
  • RBI MPC minutes due mid-week may clarify the reported 4-2 split over a shift to a calibrated-tightening stance; the US 10-year yield at about 5.1% keeps global rate pressure alive.

NMDC

Price: Rs 73.76 (as of 2026-10-05 (per Financial Express, published 9 Oct 2026)) · Cap: Large-cap Navratna PSU; India's largest iron ore producer · Horizon: 2 to 3 years

Why: Navratna PSU and among the world's lowest-cost iron ore producers. Producing over 50 MTPA with a 60 MTPA target for FY27 and 100 MTPA by FY30. Gold and silver

Bullish

Gold

Domestic 24K: Rs 14,651.40/g · Spot: $4,187.66/oz

Tactically positive. Gold fell sharply (about Rs 39,800 per 100g over four days) and has recovered for two straight sessions. A weaker dollar and Dhanteras/Diwali demand (6 and 8 Nov) support prices, though gold is already 18% costlier than last Dhanteras.

Our view: Buy on dips toward Rs 1,44,000 to Rs 1,45,000 per 10g rather than chasing peaks. For investment, prefer gold ETFs or gold mutual funds (no making charges, no purity worries); buy only BIS-hallmarked jewellery and cross-check the day's IBJA rate. Avoid unregulated digital gold for large amounts.

Drivers
  • US dollar index eased to 101.78, lowering the currency-adjusted cost of bullion for non-dollar buyers.
  • Festive and wedding demand: Dhanteras on 6 Nov and Diwali on 8 Nov 2026 are peak physical buying days in India.
  • Safe-haven demand with Brent crude near $106 and geopolitical risk elevated.
  • The Fed raised rates 25 bps in September, but the further path is uncertain, keeping opportunity-cost pressure in check.

Trading tips

  1. Risk only 1 to 2 percent of your capital on any single trade. Size positions so that a stopped-out trade is a minor cost, not a portfolio event.
  2. Always place a stop-loss when you enter a trade, and honour it. Deciding the exit after the loss starts is how small losses become large ones.
  3. Treat F&O with extra caution after the Budget 2026 STT hike: from 1 April 2026, STT is 0.05% on futures (up from 0.02%), 0.15% on options premium (up from 0.10%), and 0.15% on exercised options (up from 0.125%). Higher costs punish frequent trading, and SEBI data shows most retail F&O traders lose money.
  4. Never trade on tips from WhatsApp, Telegram or finfluencers. Verify every idea against company filings and exchange disclosures, and consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
  5. Penny stocks under Rs 100 carry manipulation and liquidity risk. Check daily volumes before buying, keep total penny exposure to 10 to 15 percent of the portfolio, and exit slowly because your own sell orders move the price.
  6. Separate investing from trading: use SIPs in diversified funds for long-term goals, keep a 6-month emergency fund untouched, and never fund trading with borrowed money.
  7. Track the macro dashboard weekly: FII/DII flows, Brent crude, USD/INR, India VIX and RBI policy signals move Indian markets more than headlines do.
