FII flows

FIIs turned net buyers on Friday 16 Oct (+Rs 1,165.92 crore, provisional NSE data), their first net-buy session after a long selling streak. The week still ended heavily negative at about -Rs 8,990 crore (third-party tracker, provisional), and FPIs have sold about Rs 3.08 lakh crore of Indian equities in 2026.

DII flows

DIIs booked profits on Friday (-Rs 3,661.28 crore) after absorbing earlier selling, but stayed net buyers for the week at about +Rs 7,813 crore (third-party tracker, provisional). DII buying remains the market's shock absorber.

What is driving the market
  • Q2FY27 IT earnings beats: TCS net profit +15% YoY to Rs 13,884 crore with AI revenue above $3.1B annualised, Tech Mahindra profit +32.7% YoY with a 1:1 bonus and Rs 15 dividend, Wipro revenue beat. The Nifty IT index rose about 11.7% on the week, its best weekly gain in 18 years.
  • Super Saturday bank results: ICICI Bank profit +20.1% beating estimates, Axis Bank +37.6-39.4%, HDFC Bank +4.7% slightly below the poll. Announced 17 Oct, they trade on Monday 19 Oct.
  • FIIs returned as net buyers on Friday (+Rs 1,165.92 crore) after sustained selling, and DIIs remain a steady liquidity cushion.
  • Rupee firmed 1.9% on the week to close at 94.96, below 95 for the first time since 8 Sep, after three RBI support measures including a dollar window for oil companies.
  • Festive demand ahead: Dhanteras (6 Nov) and Diwali (8 Nov) lifted jewellery stocks sharply on Friday, with PC Jeweller up 19%.

Key risks
  • Brent crude at $105-110 per barrel; landed cost for India estimated at $130-140. Any West Asia supply escalation would widen the import bill and inflation.
  • US September CPI came at 3.7% YoY on 14 Oct, reviving Fed rate-hike bets; the US 10-year is above 5% and the FOMC meets on 27-28 Oct.
  • Weekly FII selling is still heavy (-Rs 8,990 crore for the week) despite Friday's buying, with about Rs 3.08 lakh crore sold in 2026.
  • Narrow rally: the move is IT-led while smallcaps slipped and midcaps barely gained; a crowded IT trade is vulnerable if deal conversions disappoint.
  • Stock-specific overhangs: Yes Bank fell after the Supreme Court quashed its appeal on the Rs 8,415 crore AT1 bond write-off, and InterGlobe Aviation dropped as the Centre finalised airfare-regulation rules.

Trading tips

  1. Risk only 1 to 2 percent of your trading capital on any single trade; one bad earnings-season gap should never threaten your account.
  2. Place the stop-loss at order entry, not after the price moves against you; with VIX at 14 and event days ahead (FOMC 27-28 Oct), overnight gaps are real.
  3. F&O is more expensive since 1 April 2026: STT is 0.05% on futures, 0.15% on options premium and 0.15% on exercised options. Factor that into breakeven before sizing up; expiry-day leverage is where most retail accounts get hurt.
  4. Do not trade on tips, social media buzz or forwarded messages. Verify every claim against the exchange filing or a SEBI-registered research analyst's note.
  5. Penny stocks are thin: use limit orders, keep position sizes small, and check circuit filters and delivery percentage before entering; a 17% intraday spike can reverse just as fast.
  6. Earnings gaps cut both ways: banks reported on a Saturday and IT results have moved stocks 4 to 8% in a session. Avoid naked overnight positions into known results dates (Infosys reports 23 Oct).
  7. Keep an emergency fund completely separate from trading capital, and never trade with borrowed money; with the RBI repo at 5.50% and US yields above 5%, leverage costs more than most traders assume.
  8. Journal every trade: entry, exit, reason, and what you would do differently. Review it weekly; the journal, not the P&L, is what compounds.
