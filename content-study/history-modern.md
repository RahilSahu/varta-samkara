# Modern Indian History

## European Arrival

European powers reached India by sea in the 15th and 16th centuries, seeking trade in spices, textiles and indigo.

- **Portuguese (1498):** Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498. The Portuguese were the first Europeans to arrive by sea. Alfonso de Albuquerque captured Goa in 1510, which became the Portuguese capital in India.
- **Dutch:** The Dutch East India Company was formed in 1602. They traded from Pulicat, Surat, Cochin and Chinsurah. They were defeated by the English and faded from India by the mid-18th century.
- **English (1600):** The English East India Company was chartered in 1600 by Queen Elizabeth I. It set up factories (trading posts) at Surat (1613), Madras (Fort St George, 1639), Bombay (1668, given by Charles II) and Calcutta (Fort William, 1696).
- **French:** The French East India Company was formed in 1664. Its main settlement was Pondicherry, founded by Francois Martin in 1674. Dupleix was the most ambitious French governor.

**Carnatic Wars:** three Anglo-French wars in South India (1746-1748, 1749-1754, 1756-1763). The third was decisive: the English under Eyre Coote defeated the French at the **Battle of Wandiwash (1760)**. The **Treaty of Paris (1763)** ended French political ambitions in India; France kept only Pondicherry and a few settlements for trade.

## Plassey and Buxar: British Political Power Begins

- **Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757):** Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah, the Nawab of Bengal, near Murshidabad. Clive conspired with Mir Jafar (the Nawab's commander) and bankers like Jagat Seth. Mir Jafar was made the new Nawab. Plassey is considered the beginning of British political rule in India, not just trade.
- **Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764):** Hector Munro defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Nawab of Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Awadh) and Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor). This was a decisive military victory, unlike Plassey which was won by conspiracy.
- **Treaty of Allahabad (1765):** After Buxar, Shah Alam II granted the **Diwani** (revenue collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the Company. Robert Clive introduced the **Dual Government** in Bengal (1765-1772): the Company took revenue, the Nawab kept administration. Warren Hastings abolished Dual Government in 1772.

## British Conquest: Mysore, Marathas, Punjab

- **Anglo-Mysore Wars (four wars, 1767-1799):** Hyder Ali fought the first two; the second ended with the Treaty of Mangalore (1784) after his death in 1782. The third (1790-1792) ended with the Treaty of Seringapatam (1792). In the fourth (1799), **Tipu Sultan died fighting at Seringapatam**; the Wodeyar dynasty was restored under British protection.
- **Tipu Sultan** (r. 1782-1799): ruler of Mysore, called the **Tiger of Mysore**. Modernized his army, used rockets in warfare, planted the Tree of Liberty at Seringapatam, and corresponded with Revolutionary France. Died 4 May 1799.

- **Anglo-Maratha Wars (three wars):** First (1775-1782) ended by the **Treaty of Salbai (1782)**. Second (1803-1805) under Lord Wellesley; the **Treaty of Bassein (1802)** had already made the Peshwa a subsidiary ally. Third (1817-1819) under Lord Hastings ended the Maratha confederacy; Bombay Presidency consolidated.

- **Anglo-Sikh Wars and Punjab annexation:** After Ranjit Singh's death (1839), two wars were fought. First Anglo-Sikh War (1845-46, Treaty of Lahore); Second (1848-49). **Punjab was annexed in 1849** by Lord Dalhousie after the Battle of Gujrat.

- **Subsidiary Alliance (Lord Wellesley, 1798):** A system where an Indian ruler accepted British troops in his state, paid for their upkeep, accepted a British Resident, and gave up foreign relations. First imposed on the Nizam of Hyderabad (1798). States affected included Awadh, Mysore, Tanjore, the Peshwa and the Gaekwad.
- **Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie):** States whose rulers died without a natural male heir would lapse to the British. Applied to **Satara (1848), Jaitpur and Sambalpur (1849), Baghat (1850), Udaipur (1852), Jhansi (1854), Nagpur (1854)** and Awadh (annexed 1856 on grounds of misrule, not lapse).

## Economic Impact of British Rule

- **Deindustrialization:** British machine-made goods flooded India while Indian handloom exports were taxed heavily in Britain. Traditional industries (textiles, handicrafts) collapsed, especially after 1813 when the Company's trade monopoly ended and one-way free trade began.
- Dadabhai Naoroji propounded the **Drain Theory** in *Poverty and UnBritish Rule in India* (1901): Britain drained India's wealth through salaries, pensions, interest and trade surplus. He is called the **Grand Old Man of India** and was the first Indian Member of the British Parliament (1892, Finsbury Central).

**Land revenue systems:**

| System | Introduced by | Year | Where | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent Settlement | Lord Cornwallis | 1793 | Bengal, Bihar, Orissa | Zamindars made permanent owners; revenue fixed at 10/11 to the Company, 1/11 to the zamindar |
| Ryotwari | Thomas Munro, Alexander Read | 1820s | Madras, Bombay, parts of Assam | Settlement made directly with the ryot (peasant); no middleman |
| Mahalwari | Holt Mackenzie, Lord William Bentinck | 1833 | North-West Provinces, Punjab, Central India | Settlement with the village (mahal) as a unit; village headman collected revenue |

## Social and Religious Reform Movements

- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772-1833):** Called the **Father of Modern India** and the **Father of the Indian Renaissance**. Founded the **Brahmo Sabha in 1828** (later Brahmo Samaj). Campaigned against sati, child marriage and caste rigidities. His efforts led Lord William Bentinck to abolish **sati in 1829** (Regulation XVII).
- **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820-1891):** Championed widow remarriage; the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act** was passed in **1856**. He also pushed for women's education and against polygamy and child marriage.
- **Jyotiba Phule (1827-1890):** Founded the **Satyashodhak Samaj** in **1873** in Maharashtra to fight caste discrimination and Brahmin dominance. With his wife **Savitribai Phule**, he opened schools for girls and lower castes; Savitribai is regarded as India's first woman teacher.
- **Dayananda Saraswati (1824-1883):** Founded the **Arya Samaj in 1875** (Bombay; headquarters later at Lahore). Call: **"Back to the Vedas."** Rejected idol worship, caste by birth and child marriage; supported Vedic education (DAV schools and colleges came from this movement).
- Swami Vivekananda (1863-1902): disciple of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa. Addressed the **Parliament of the World's Religions at Chicago in 1893**; founded the **Ramakrishna Mission in 1897** for social service.
- Other movements: **Prarthana Samaj** (1867, Bombay, joined by M.G. Ranade); **Aligarh Movement** by **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** (MAO College, 1875; became AMU in 1920).

## The Revolt of 1857

The first large-scale uprising against British rule. It began at **Meerut on 10 May 1857** when sepoys refused the new Enfield rifle cartridges rumored to be greased with cow and pig fat. **Mangal Pandey** had revolted earlier at Barrackpore on 29 March 1857 and was executed on 8 April 1857.

**Causes:** political (Doctrine of Lapse, annexation of Awadh), economic (heavy land revenue, destruction of handicrafts), military (low pay, denial of promotion, overseas service, cartridge issue) and social-religious (fear of conversion, interference in customs).

**Centers and leaders:**

| Center | Leader |
|---|---|
| Delhi | Bahadur Shah II (Zafar), proclaimed Emperor; real command with Bakht Khan |
| Lucknow | Begum Hazrat Mahal (her son Birjis Qadir declared Nawab) |
| Kanpur | Nana Sahib (adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II); Tantia Tope his general |
| Jhansi | Rani Lakshmibai |
| Bihar (Jagdispur) | Kunwar Singh (Veer Kunwar Singh), aged about 80 |
| Bareilly | Khan Bahadur Khan |
| Faizabad | Maulvi Ahmadullah |

**Why it failed:** no central leadership or common plan; limited to North and Central India (South India, Punjab and most princely states stayed aloof or helped the British); the British had superior arms, railways and telegraph; Indian leaders fought separately and were divided.

**Results:** the **Government of India Act 1858** ended Company rule; the Crown took direct control. **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 November 1858**, read at Allahabad by Lord Canning) promised no further annexations, religious non-interference, and equal treatment in public employment. Canning became the first Viceroy. The Indian army was reorganized with a higher ratio of British to Indian soldiers.

## Indian National Congress and Early Nationalism

- **Founding (1885):** The Indian National Congress was founded in **December 1885** at Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College). First session president: **W.C. Bonnerjee**. **72 delegates** attended. **A.O. Hume**, a retired British civil servant, is regarded as its founder.
- **Moderates vs Extremists:** Moderates (Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee) used petitions and constitutional methods. Extremists (Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai: **Lal-Bal-Pal**) demanded Swaraj and used boycott and mass agitation. Tilak's slogan: **"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it."**
- **Partition of Bengal (1905):** Lord Curzon divided Bengal in **October 1905** on administrative grounds; nationalists saw it as divide and rule. It sparked the **Swadeshi Movement** (1905-1908): boycott of British goods, promotion of Indian goods, national education (Bengal National College), and Bande Mataram as the rallying song. Bengal was reunited in **1911** (announced at the Delhi Durbar; capital shifted from Calcutta to Delhi).
- **Surat Split (1907):** Congress split into Moderates and Extremists at the Surat session.
- **Muslim League (1906):** Founded at **Dhaka in December 1906**; Aga Khan was the first president. It demanded separate electorates, granted by the **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)**.
- **Lucknow Pact (1916):** Congress and the Muslim League agreed on joint demands; Congress accepted separate electorates.

## Revolutionary Phase

**First phase (1905-1915):**
- Bengal: **Anushilan Samiti**; **Khudiram Bose** and Prafulla Chaki attempted to kill Kingsford at **Muzaffarpur (1908)**; Khudiram was hanged.
- Maharashtra: **Abhinav Bharat** (Vinayak Savarkar); **Nasik conspiracy (1909)**, Jackson murdered.
- Punjab: **Ghadar Party** founded **1913** at San Francisco by **Lala Har Dayal**; Komagata Maru incident (1914).
- Delhi conspiracy (1912): bomb thrown at Lord Hardinge by **Rash Behari Bose**.

**Second phase: HSRA (1920s):**
- **Hindustan Republican Association** founded **1924** by Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan and others; renamed **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)** in 1928 under **Chandrashekhar Azad**.
- **Kakori Conspiracy (9 August 1925):** train robbery near Lucknow; **Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Roshan Singh and Rajendra Lahiri** were hanged (1927).
- **Saunders murder (17 December 1928):** **Bhagat Singh, Azad and Rajguru** killed police officer Saunders to avenge Lala Lajpat Rai's death (lathi charge during anti-Simon protests).
- **Assembly bomb (8 April 1929):** **Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt** threw bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly to "make the deaf hear"; they courted arrest.
- **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev** were hanged on **23 March 1931** in the Lahore Conspiracy Case. **Chandrashekhar Azad** died in a police encounter at Alfred Park, Allahabad, on **27 February 1931**.
- Bengal revolutionaries: **Surya Sen** led the **Chittagong Armoury Raid (18 April 1930)**; **Pritilata Waddedar** died in the Pahartali raid (1932).

## Gandhian Phase I: 1917-1922

Gandhi returned to India in **January 1915** from South Africa. Gopal Krishna Gokhale was his political mentor.

- **Champaran Satyagraha (1917):** Gandhi's first satyagraha in India; indigo planters (tinkathia system) in Bihar. Success: the tinkathia system was abolished.
- **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918):** Gandhi fasted for textile workers demanding a wage hike; workers got a 35 percent increase.
- **Kheda Satyagraha (1918):** peasants in Gujarat demanded revenue remission after crop failure; Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel emerged as a leader here.
- **Rowlatt Act (1919):** allowed detention without trial for two years; called the **Black Act**. Gandhi called a nationwide hartal on 6 April 1919.
- **Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919):** General **Dyer** ordered firing on an unarmed gathering at Amritsar on Baisakhi day; hundreds killed (official figure 379). **Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood** in protest.
- **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922):** launched August 1920 after the Khilafat issue, Punjab wrongs and Swaraj demand. Program: boycott of foreign goods, schools, courts and councils; promotion of khadi. **Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922):** a mob killed 22 policemen at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP); Gandhi **withdrew the movement** on 12 February 1922.

## Gandhian Phase II: 1922-1939

- **Simon Commission (1927):** an all-white commission to review the 1919 Act; boycotted with the slogan **"Simon, go back."** Lala Lajpat Rai died of injuries from a lathi charge at Lahore (1928).
- **Lahore Session (1929):** Congress under president **Jawaharlal Nehru** adopted **Purna Swaraj** (complete independence) as the goal; **26 January 1930** observed as Independence Day.
- **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930):** began with the **Dandi March (12 March - 6 April 1930)**: Gandhi walked about 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi and broke the salt law on 6 April 1930. Salt satyagraha, boycott of foreign cloth and liquor, refusal to pay taxes.
- **Round Table Conferences (London):** First (1930-31, Congress boycotted); Second (1931, Gandhi attended as sole Congress representative); Third (1932).
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931):** Irwin agreed to release political prisoners and allow salt-making; Gandhi agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and attend the Second Round Table Conference.
- **Communal Award (1932):** Ramsay MacDonald granted separate electorates to depressed classes; Gandhi fasted in Yerwada Jail; the **Poona Pact (September 1932)** between Gandhi and Ambedkar gave reserved seats instead of separate electorates.
- **Government of India Act 1935:** provided provincial autonomy and an all-India federation (federation never came into force); elections of 1937 brought Congress ministries to power in most provinces.

## Gandhian Phase III: 1939-1947

- **World War II (1939):** Congress ministries resigned (1939) over India being dragged into the war without consultation.
- **Cripps Mission (1942):** offered dominion status after the war; Gandhi called it a **"post-dated cheque on a crashing bank."** Rejected by all parties.
- **Quit India Movement (1942):** launched **8 August 1942** at Bombay (Gowalia Tank); Gandhi's call: **"Do or Die."** Leaders were arrested the next morning; Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the flag. Parallel governments formed at Ballia, Satara (Prati Sarkar) and Tamluk. Suppressed within months but marked the point of no return.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA:** Bose escaped house arrest (1941), reached Japan via Germany, and took over the **Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj)**; his calls were **"Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom"** and **"Jai Hind."** The INA fought alongside Japan at Imphal and Kohima (1944).
- **Naval Mutiny (February 1946):** ratings of HMIS Talwar at Bombay mutinied over poor food and racial discrimination; spread to Karachi and other ports; Vallabhbhai Patel and Jinnah persuaded them to surrender.
- **Cabinet Mission (1946):** proposed a three-tier federation with grouping of provinces; accepted by Congress and League initially, then broke down over grouping. **Direct Action Day (16 August 1946)** called by the Muslim League led to the Great Calcutta Killings.

## Constitutional Developments

| Act / Event | Year | Key provisions |
|---|---|---|
| Indian Councils Act (Morley-Minto) | 1909 | Separate electorates for Muslims; enlarged councils |
| Government of India Act (Montagu-Chelmsford) | 1919 | Dyarchy in provinces (reserved vs transferred subjects); bicameral central legislature |
| Government of India Act | 1935 | Provincial autonomy; federal scheme; RBI established (1935) under this Act |
| Cripps Mission | 1942 | Dominion status after war; rejected |
| Cabinet Mission | 1946 | Constituent Assembly; three-tier federation plan |
| Mountbatten Plan | 3 June 1947 | Partition plan; princely states free to join India or Pakistan |
| Indian Independence Act | 1947 | Passed July 1947; independence on 15 August 1947; two dominions created |

- The **Constituent Assembly** first met on **9 December 1946**; Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was the interim president; **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** became permanent president on 11 December 1946. **B.R. Ambedkar** chaired the Drafting Committee (formed 29 August 1947).

## Independence, Partition and Integration

- **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947):** partition of British India into India and Pakistan; Punjab and Bengal to be divided; referendum in NWFP and Sylhet; princely states could accede to either dominion.
- **Indian Independence Act 1947:** received royal assent 18 July 1947; India and Pakistan became independent dominions on **15 August 1947** (Pakistan's independence is observed on 14 August). Lord Mountbatten became the first Governor-General of independent India; **Jawaharlal Nehru** became the first Prime Minister. Gandhi was in Calcutta/Noakhali working for communal peace.
- **Integration of princely states:** **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (Home Minister) with **V.P. Menon** integrated over 560 princely states using the Instrument of Accession.
  - **Junagadh:** Muslim Nawab acceded to Pakistan against a Hindu-majority population; Indian troops moved in and a plebiscite (February 1948) favored India.
  - **Hyderabad:** the Nizam resisted; **Operation Polo** (13-18 September 1948) integrated Hyderabad into India.
  - **Kashmir:** Maharaja Hari Singh signed the Instrument of Accession on **26 October 1947** after tribal invaders backed by Pakistan attacked; Indian troops were airlifted to Srinagar on 27 October 1947.
- The Constitution of India was adopted on **26 November 1949** and came into force on **26 January 1950**.
