# Medieval Indian History (c. 700 to 1700 CE)

Medieval India runs roughly from 700 CE to 1700 CE: regional kingdoms after Harsha, the Delhi Sultanate, Vijayanagara, the Mughals and the Marathas.

## Early Medieval North: The Three Powers

After Harsha's death (647 CE), three powers fought the **Tripartite Struggle** for **Kannauj**: Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas and Rashtrakutas.

| Dynasty | Region | Capital | Key ruler and fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gurjara-Pratihara | North/West India | Kannauj | Nagabhata I (r. c. 730-756) checked Arab invasions from Sindh (c. 738); Mihira Bhoja (r. c. 836-885) was the greatest Pratihara |
| Pala | Bengal, Bihar | Pataliputra | Gopala (r. c. 750-770) founded it by election; Dharmapala (r. c. 770-810) founded Vikramashila university and Somapura Mahavihara (Paharpur) |
| Rashtrakuta | Deccan | Manyakheta | Krishna I built the Kailasanatha temple at Ellora; Amoghavarsha I (r. c. 814-878) wrote Kavirajamarga, the earliest Kannada work |
| Chola | Tamil Nadu | Thanjavur | Rajaraja I and Rajendra I; naval power; see below |
| Chandela | Bundelkhand | Khajuraho | Khajuraho temples (10th c.) |
| Solanki/Chaulukya | Gujarat | Anhilwara | Patronized the Dilwara Jain temples |
| Eastern Ganga | Odisha | Cuttack | Narasimhadeva I built the Konark Sun temple (13th c.) |

## Chola Administration

The Cholas (9th-13th c.) had the most developed local administration of the period.

- **Rajaraja I** (985-1014) built the **Brihadeeswara temple** at Thanjavur; **Rajendra I** (1014-1044) founded **Gangaikondacholapuram** and sent a naval expedition against Srivijaya (Southeast Asia).
- Units: **Mandalam** (province), **Valanadu** (district), **Nadu** (sub-district).
- Village bodies: **Ur** (general assembly), **Sabha** (Brahmin assembly), **Nagaram** (merchant town assembly). The **Kudavolai** election-by-lot system is recorded in the **Uttaramerur inscriptions**.

## Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526)

Five dynasties: Slave (1206-1290), Khalji (1290-1320), Tughlaq (1320-1414), Sayyid (1414-1451), Lodi (1451-1526).

**Slave dynasty.** Qutub-ud-din Aibak (1206-1210) began the **Qutub Minar**, built the **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque and **Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer), and died from a polo fall. Iltutmish (1211-1236) moved the capital to Delhi, issued the silver **tanka** and copper **jital**, formed the **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** (Chalisa, 40 nobles), started the **iqta** system and nominated his daughter **Razia** (1236-1240), the only woman on the Delhi throne. Balban (1266-1287) preached divine kingship (**Zil-i-Ilahi**), crushed the Chalisa, imposed court rituals **Sijda** and **Paibos**, and created the **Diwan-i-Arz** (military department).

**Khalji dynasty.** Alauddin Khalji (1296-1316): market reforms with fixed prices under the **Shahna-i-Mandi** and **Diwan-i-Riyasat**; cash salaries for soldiers; **dagh** (horse branding) and **chehra** (soldier rolls). His general **Malik Kafur** campaigned in the Deccan (Devagiri, Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Madurai). Alauddin built the **Alai Darwaza** and the city of Siri.

**Tughlaq dynasty.** Ghiyasuddin (1320-1325) built **Tughlaqabad**. Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325-1351) is known for failed experiments: shifting the capital to **Daulatabad**, token copper currency, heavy Doab taxation; he set up **Diwan-i-Kohi** (agriculture). **Ibn Battuta** visited his court and wrote the *Rehla*. Firoz Shah (1351-1388) was a welfare ruler: founded **Firozabad** and Jaunpur, built canals, imposed **jizya on Brahmins**.

**Sayyids and Lodis.** Khizr Khan founded the Sayyid line (1414-1451). The Lodis (1451-1526) were the first Afghan dynasty: Sikandar Lodi founded **Agra** (1504); **Ibrahim Lodi** fell to Babur at **Panipat (1526)**.

### Sultanate administration: key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Iqta | Revenue assignment to nobles (muqtis) for military service |
| Khalisa | Crown land; revenue went straight to the Sultan |
| Kharaj | Land tax on non-Muslims |
| Ushr | Land tax on Muslims |
| Jizya | Tax on non-Muslim subjects |
| Zakat | Muslim religious tax (2.5 percent of wealth) |
| Khams | One-fifth of war booty for the state |
| Wazir | Finance head (Diwan-i-Wizarat) |
| Diwan-i-Risalat | Religious affairs and appeals |
| Diwan-i-Insha | Royal correspondence |
| Qazi-ul-Quzat | Chief judge |

## Vijayanagara Empire (1336-1646)

- Founded **1336** by **Harihara I and Bukka Raya I** (Sangama dynasty) with saint **Vidyaranya's** blessings; capital **Vijayanagara (Hampi)** on the **Tungabhadra**.
- **Krishnadevaraya** (1509-1529, Tuluva dynasty): greatest king; wrote **Amuktamalyada** in Telugu; court had the **Ashtadiggajas** (eight poets); **Domingo Paes** visited then.
- **Battle of Talikota (1565)**: Deccan sultanates' alliance defeated and sacked the city; end of its greatness.
- Administration: **mandalams** (provinces) under **nayaks**; the **Amara-nayaka system** gave commanders land for troops. **Virupaksha** and **Vittala** (stone chariot) temples stand at Hampi (UNESCO site).

## Bahmani Kingdom and Deccan Sultanates

- Founded **1347** by **Ala-ud-din Hasan Bahman Shah** (Hasan Gangu); capitals **Gulbarga**, then **Bidar**. Minister **Mahmud Gawan** built a famous madrasa at Bidar.
- After its breakup (c. 1527), five sultanates: **Bijapur** (Adil Shahi), **Ahmadnagar** (Nizam Shahi), **Golconda** (Qutb Shahi), **Berar** (Imad Shahi), **Bidar** (Barid Shahi).
- **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** built **Charminar** (1591) and founded Hyderabad.

## Bhakti and Sufi Movements

Bhakti (devotion to a personal god) rose in Tamil Nadu with the **Nayanars** (Shaivite) and **Alvars** (Vaishnavite), then spread north.

| Saint | Tradition | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| Shankaracharya (8th c.) | Advaita Vedanta | Founded four mathas: Sringeri, Puri, Dwarka, Badrinath |
| Kabir | Nirguna Bhakti | Weaver-poet; dohas in the *Bijak*; rejected Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy |
| Guru Nanak (1469-1539) | Sikhism | Founder; teachings in the *Adi Granth* (compiled 1604 by Guru Arjan) |
| Mirabai | Saguna (Krishna) | Mewar princess; Krishna bhajans |
| Tulsidas | Saguna (Rama) | *Ramcharitmanas* in Awadhi; contemporary of Akbar |
| Surdas | Saguna (Krishna) | *Sursagar* |
| Chaitanya (1486-1534) | Gaudiya Vaishnavism | Bengal; popularized sankirtan |

**Sufi orders.** The **Chishti** order began in India with **Muinuddin Chishti** (dargah at Ajmer; arrived c. 1192); later **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Delhi). His disciple **Amir Khusrau** was a Persian poet linked to early Urdu/Hindavi verse and qawwali. The **Suhrawardi** order centered on **Bahauddin Zakariya** at Multan.

## Mughal Empire

**Babur (1526-1530).** Won **Panipat (21 April 1526)** over Ibrahim Lodi with artillery and the **tulughma** flanking tactic; then **Khanwa (1527)** over Rana Sanga, **Chanderi (1528)** over Medini Rai, **Ghaghra (1529)** over the Afghans. Wrote the **Baburnama** in Turkish.

**Humayun and Sher Shah.** Humayun (1530-1540, 1555-1556) lost to **Sher Shah Suri** at **Chausa (1539)** and **Kannauj (1540)**, regained the throne in 1555, died 1556. Sher Shah (1540-1545) built the **Sadak-i-Azam** (Grand Trunk Road), issued the silver **rupiya**, reformed revenue by measurement, and died at **Kalinjar (1545)**.

**Akbar (1556-1605).** Regent **Bairam Khan** won **Second Panipat (1556)** over Hemu. Mansabdari system: **zat** (personal rank) and **sawar** (cavalry rank). **Zabt/dahsala** revenue on a ten-year average, run by **Todar Mal**. Abolished pilgrimage tax (1563) and **jizya (1564)**. Rajput policy of marriage alliances and high mansabs (Mewar stayed out). Built **Fatehpur Sikri** and the **Ibadat Khana**; proclaimed **Din-i-Ilahi (1582)**; court had the **Navratnas** (Birbal, Tansen, Todar Mal, Abul Fazl, Faizi, Man Singh, Rahim among them). **Abul Fazl** wrote the **Akbarnama** and **Ain-i-Akbari**.

**Jahangir (1605-1627).** Hung the **Zanjir-i-Adl** (chain of justice); **Nur Jahan** became the power behind the throne (married 1611). Executed **Guru Arjan (1606)**. Englishmen **Hawkins (1608)** and **Sir Thomas Roe (1615)** visited his court.

**Shah Jahan (1628-1658).** Built the **Taj Mahal**, **Red Fort**, **Jama Masjid** (Delhi) and the **Peacock Throne**; founded **Shahjahanabad**. Deposed by Aurangzeb in 1658.

**Aurangzeb (1658-1707).** Longest Mughal reign; called **Zinda Pir**. Reimposed **jizya (1679)**. Annexed **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)**; spent his last decades fighting Marathas in the Deccan. Executed **Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675)** at Delhi and **Sambhaji (1689)** at Tulapur.

## Marathas

**Shivaji (1627-1680).** Crowned **Chhatrapati at Raigad (1674)**. Council of eight: the **Ashtapradhan** (Peshwa, Amatya, Sachiv, Mantri, Senapati, Sumant, Nyayadhish, Panditrao). Signed the **Treaty of Purandar (1665)** with Jai Singh, then escaped from Agra (1666). Revenue from **chauth** (one-fourth) and **sardeshmukhi** (one-tenth). Hill forts: Raigad, Pratapgarh, Sinhagad, Panhala.

**Later Marathas.** Sambhaji (1680-1689), Rajaram, regent Tarabai, then Shahu (1708). **Balaji Vishwanath** (1713-1720) was the first Bhat-family Peshwa. **Bajirao I** (1720-1740) won **Palkhed (1728)** against the Nizam by maneuver. **Third Panipat (14 January 1761)**: Marathas under **Sadashivrao Bhau** lost to **Ahmad Shah Abdali**, ending hopes of all-India supremacy.

## Regional States

- **Rana Sanga** of Mewar led the Rajput confederacy against Babur; defeated at **Khanwa (1527)**.
- **Maharana Pratap** fought Akbar's general **Man Singh** at **Haldighati (1576)**, then kept up guerrilla resistance from the Aravallis and never submitted.
- **Lachit Borphukan** of the Ahoms beat the Mughals under Ram Singh at **Saraighat (1671)** on the Brahmaputra.
