Three New Judges Take Oath at Supreme Court, Working Strength Rises to 37

Three new judges were sworn in to the Supreme Court on Tuesday, taking the apex court's working strength to 37, one short of its sanctioned strength of 38. Their appointments had been notified by the Centre on Monday.

Union Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal announced that President Droupadi Murmu had appointed Justice Sunita Agarwal, Chief Justice of the Gujarat High Court; Justice Devendra Kumar Upadhyaya, Chief Justice of the Delhi High Court; and Justice Aparesh Kumar Singh, Chief Justice of the Telangana High Court, as judges of the Supreme Court.

## The three new judges

Justice Sunita Agarwal, whose parent High Court is Allahabad, has served as Chief Justice of the Gujarat High Court since July 2023. Her elevation makes her the third woman judge on the Supreme Court, alongside Justices B.V. Nagarathna and V. Mohan.

Justice Devendra Kumar Upadhyaya, also from the Allahabad High Court, became a judge in 2011 after serving as Uttar Pradesh's Chief Standing Counsel. He headed the Bombay High Court as Chief Justice from July 2023 before being transferred to the Delhi High Court in January 2025.

Justice Aparesh Kumar Singh practised before the Patna and Jharkhand High Courts before becoming a judge in 2012. He served as Chief Justice of the Tripura High Court before taking charge as Chief Justice of the Telangana High Court in July 2025.

## Collegium recommendation and expanded strength

The Supreme Court Collegium, led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, had recommended the three names for elevation on September 28, 2026. The sanctioned strength of the apex court was recently increased from 34 to 38 under the Supreme Court Amendment Act, 2026, to account for the growing workload.
