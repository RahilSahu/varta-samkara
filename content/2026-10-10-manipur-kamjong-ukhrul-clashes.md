Three Killed in Fresh Clashes in Manipur's Kamjong and Ukhrul Districts

Three people, including a woman, were killed in fresh clashes between armed groups in Manipur's Kamjong and Ukhrul districts over the past 24 hours, officials said on Saturday. The violence also spread to parts of Kangpokpi district, where a heavy exchange of fire between armed groups was reported, and several houses were torched.

In Kamjong district, a 42-year-old mother of five, Domkhonei Touthang, was shot near her neck in Chassad village on Friday night and died within minutes, according to officials. Her killing triggered an exchange of fire between armed Kuki and Naga groups that continued into Saturday morning. A young man, Michael Ngamminlen Haokip, was killed in the ensuing clashes, during which 10 houses were also reportedly set ablaze.

## Prohibitory orders in Chassad

District authorities imposed prohibitory orders under Section 162 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) within a 400-metre radius of the Chassad police gate junction, banning gatherings of five or more people. Reports on social media that the Kamjong police station had been set on fire were dismissed by the police.

In a separate incident in Ukhrul district, a 40-year-old man, Sandeep Rai, was shot dead near Chepu Yaolen after suspected militants opened fire at his vehicle. He died inside the four-wheeler he was driving, officials said. A heavy exchange of fire was also reported between armed groups at Makui Ashang and Tapon along the IT Road in Kangpokpi district.

## Official response and tribal bodies' call

Deputy Chief Minister Nemcha Kipgen condemned the killings from Kangpokpi on October 10, calling for thorough and impartial investigations and appealing for restraint to avoid retaliatory violence. "I urge the authorities to conduct thorough and impartial investigations and bring those responsible to justice," she said, stressing that the protection of every civilian life must remain paramount.

Meanwhile, the Kuki Inpi Manipur, the apex body of the Kuki tribes, condemned what it described as coordinated attacks on Kuki-Zo civilians and alleged in a press statement that armed elements carried out the attacks in collusion with state security personnel. Kuki civil society organisations announced shutdowns along National Highways 202 and 2 in protest.

More than 40 people have been killed since violence between Kuki and Naga communities broke out in February this year, with the unrest concentrated in Ukhrul, Kamjong, Kangpokpi, Tamenglong and Noney districts and parts of Senapati district.
