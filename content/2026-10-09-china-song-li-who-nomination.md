China Nominates Health Official Song Li to Lead the WHO

China on Thursday formally nominated Song Li, a senior public health official specialising in maternal and child health, to become the next Director-General of the World Health Organization. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning confirmed the nomination at a briefing in Beijing.

If elected, Song would succeed Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, whose second five-year term ends on August 15, 2027; he cannot seek a third term under WHO rules. The World Health Assembly will appoint the next director-general in May 2027 after the Executive Board shortlists up to three candidates, with a candidates' forum scheduled for November 18. The nomination deadline was September 24, and other publicly identified candidates come from Saudi Arabia, Belgium, Qatar, Botswana, Indonesia and Spain.

Song has spent more than two decades in China's health system, rising through the maternal and child health administration. Her career covered clinical medicine and public health, with a master's from Shandong Medical University, a PhD from Peking University, and a visiting scholarship at Harvard University. She worked on responses to SARS, avian influenza and Covid-19. Mao Ning said China believed Song would help the WHO play a more active role in improving global health. The nomination comes as the WHO faces funding pressures after the US withdrawal under President Trump and as China has pledged to increase its financial support.
