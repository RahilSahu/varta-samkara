Americans Robert Hall and Michael Woodford Win 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics

American academics Robert Hall and Michael Woodford won the 2026 Nobel prize in economics on Monday for work on the microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences said.

## Why the work matters

The academy said their models, now used by central banks and finance ministries, help explain how decisions by households and firms shape inflation, consumption and output, and how clear communication on future policy can influence expectations. Committee chair John Hassler said the work helps policymakers deal with shocks such as energy crises or sudden changes in household consumption.

## Prize details

The award, formally the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, is worth 12 million Swedish crowns and is the final Nobel prize announced this year. Hall is a professor at Stanford University and Woodford is a professor at Columbia University. The prize was first awarded in 1969 and was established by Sweden central bank, separate from the original five Nobel prizes.
