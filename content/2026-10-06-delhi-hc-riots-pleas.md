Delhi HC Dismisses Pleas Seeking SIT Probe, FIRs Against Politicians Over 2020 Riots

The Delhi High Court on Monday dismissed a batch of petitions seeking a Special Investigation Team (SIT) probe into the 2020 northeast Delhi riots and the 2019 violence at Jamia Millia Islamia following anti-CAA protests. A bench of Justices Vivek Chaudhary and Manoj Jain said, "For the reasons mentioned in the judgment, all the petitions are dismissed."

The bench noted that the batch was divided into two parts, one concerning the northeast Delhi riots and another concerning the Jamia violence. A detailed judgment is awaited. The court had reserved its verdict on the petitions on July 24.

## What the petitions sought

The petitions sought directions to set up an SIT, commission of inquiry or fact-finding committee independent of the police and the central government, along with medical treatment for victims, compensation and registration of FIRs against erring police officers.

Several pleas demanded that political leaders be booked for alleged hate speeches. Petitioner Shaikh Mujtaba sought FIRs against BJP leaders Kapil Mishra, Anurag Thakur, Parvesh Verma and Abhay Verma over their speeches. The group Lawyers Voice sought FIRs over alleged hate speeches by leaders including Sonia Gandhi, Rahul Gandhi, Priyanka Gandhi Vadra, Manish Sisodia, Amanatullah Khan, Akbaruddin Owaisi and Waris Pathan. CPI(M) leader Brinda Karat sought an independent probe into alleged excesses by police and Rapid Action Force personnel, while Ajay Gautam sought an NIA probe into the alleged funding of anti-CAA protests.

## Background

A communal clash broke out in northeast Delhi on February 24, 2020, amid protests against the citizenship law, leaving at least 53 people dead and around 700 injured. The other petitions related to the violence that broke out in Jamia Millia Islamia in December 2019, where petitioners claimed students were subjected to police brutalities and argued for an independent investigation.

The Supreme Court had earlier asked the High Court to decide one of the pleas expeditiously.
