WMO Warns El Nino Will Strengthen Before December Peak, Among Strongest Since 1950

This year's El Nino will intensify before peaking in December and is nearly 100 per cent certain to last until February 2027, the World Meteorological Organization said in its monthly Global Seasonal Climate Update on Thursday, warning it could rank among the strongest events since at least 1950.

The UN weather and climate agency said sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific, the region at the heart of the El Nino phenomenon, were forecast to reach a record 3.7 degrees Celsius above normal from October to December, well above the previous record anomaly of 2.6C from November 2015 to January 2016. The forecast rests on consensus across 13 leading national and international meteorological centres. WMO chief Celeste Saulo said El Nino was "firmly established and strengthening rapidly", and noted it releases stored ocean heat without adding new heat to the planet, though the ocean it is drawing from is the warmest ever measured.

## Drought and flood risks worldwide

The WMO said the event is likely to bring above-average temperatures to much of the globe and shift rainfall patterns, increasing the risk of drought and floods in different regions, compounding the effects of man-made climate change. The agency flagged increased drought risk in southern Africa, with countries from Brazil to India also vulnerable. In India, the prevailing El Nino has already contributed to a 53.4 per cent June-August rainfall deficit that prompted Andhra Pradesh's early drought declaration.
