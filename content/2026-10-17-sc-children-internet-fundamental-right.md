Supreme Court Rules Children Have Fundamental Right to Internet Access

The Supreme Court has held that children, like any other individual, have a fundamental right to access and use the internet, declaring that denying it altogether would be "absurd". The ruling was delivered by a Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and Vinod K. Chandran.

## The ruling

The Bench said: "We have no hesitation in saying that the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right of every individual under Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution. Our children, irrespective of their age enjoy the same rights, and to deny the same altogether would be as absurd."

The court cautioned that blanket restrictions on children's internet access in the name of safety risk undermining their freedom, privacy, and ability to seek help when they encounter online abuse.

## Why it matters

The order extends the existing constitutional interpretation that internet access is a fundamental right to minors, a group over which schools, parents, and authorities frequently impose restrictions. Legal experts say the ruling could shape how internet shutdowns and content blocks affecting children are scrutinised.
