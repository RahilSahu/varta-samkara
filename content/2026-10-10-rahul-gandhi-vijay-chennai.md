Rahul Gandhi Meets Tamil Nadu CM Vijay, Addresses Students at Chennai's Island Grounds

Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha Rahul Gandhi visited Chennai on Saturday for a lunch meeting with Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay and an evening interaction with students at Island Grounds, his first in-person engagement with the Chief Minister since the Secular Social Justice Victory Alliance was formed. The alliance comprises the TVK, Congress and VCK.

Gandhi arrived at Chennai airport on Saturday morning and met Vijay at his Neelankarai residence for lunch at around 1.30 pm. He then proceeded to Island Grounds for the "Maanavargalin Kural" (Chhatron Ki Goonj, or Voice of Students) programme in the evening. Tamil Nadu Congress Committee president Manickam Tagore said the interaction would cover issues such as NEET, lack of jobs, education for girls and women, and the Union government's education policy.

## Students' outreach and heavy security

According to organisers, more than one lakh students registered for the event through a website and QR codes circulated across Tamil Nadu, though entry to the main interaction was restricted to 10,000 to 20,000 students who had received QR codes and identification cards. The programme follows similar student outreach editions held in Kota in Rajasthan, Pune and Nashik in Maharashtra, and Indore in Madhya Pradesh.

Chennai was placed under a five-tier security blanket with about 15,000 police personnel led by Commissioner A. Amalraj, also covering VVIP transit routes. PTI reported that access to the event was restricted to QR-code entry, with gender-segregated entry corridors and the general public excluded from the venue.

## Absence from Delhi protest draws questions

Gandhi's visit coincided with a heavy police crackdown in Delhi on a planned youth protest demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. Left supporters questioned the Congress leader's absence from the capital on the day. TNCC chief Tagore said Gandhi had committed to the Chennai programme earlier, and Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge issued a strong public condemnation of the detentions, saying the party would defend the protesters' right to dissent.
