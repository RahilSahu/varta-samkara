EU and China Agree to Halve Chinese Hybrid Vehicle Exports, Ease Rare-Earth Curbs

The European Union and China have reached a "shared understanding" that would cut Chinese exports of hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles to the EU by more than half, European Trade Commissioner Maros Sefcovic announced in Beijing on Friday after two days of trade talks aimed at rebalancing the bloc's ballooning deficit with China.

The understanding would "moderate" China's exports of hybrids and plug-in hybrids by several million cars over a four-year period, Sefcovic said, though he declined to detail how the arrangement would be enforced, saying only that it had been negotiated in detail, complies with WTO rules, and would first be presented to EU leaders at their summit in Brussels next week. China's own statement confirmed an understanding on hybrid trade but gave no figures or mechanism.

## A package with market access and rare earths

Beyond vehicles, the package gives European exporters better access to China's market. Beijing agreed to reduce import tariffs on seven product categories covering nearly 4 billion euros of European exports, including car parts, olive oil, footwear and textiles, with Sefcovic estimating savings of at least 225 million euros in import duties.

On rare earths, China committed to keep facilitating export licence approvals for rare earths and permanent magnets through a "green channel" fast-track mechanism, making access smoother and more predictable for European firms while leaving Beijing's export controls in place. The sides also agreed to give advance warning of export controls and to improve the transparency of listing and delisting procedures.

## "A crucial first step, but only a first step"

"This is far from the end. It is a crucial first step, but only a first step, in the process of rebalancing," Sefcovic told reporters, noting that the EU's trade deficit with China amounted to over 1 billion euros ($1.12 billion) a day. He added that this was the first time China had accepted to moderate its exports without first going through trade tensions.

Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao, who led the talks with Sefcovic over three months of negotiations, said that "China is not the root cause of the EU's problems, but a partner in resolving them," while expressing concern over recent EU trade restrictions, according to the Chinese commerce ministry.

Outstanding issues remain. Both sides agreed to keep analysing price commitments in the EU's anti-subsidy investigation into Chinese electric vehicles and to jointly study lifting restrictions on meat products from EU member states affected by blue tongue disease, with further talks set for March next year. Business groups welcomed the outcome: the China Chamber of Commerce to the EU called the results "pragmatic," and the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association described the announcement as "a positive step in easing trade tensions."
