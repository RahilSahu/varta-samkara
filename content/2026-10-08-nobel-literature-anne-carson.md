Canadian Poet Anne Carson Wins 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature was awarded on Thursday to Canadian poet and essayist Anne Carson, "for her bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature", the Swedish Academy announced in Stockholm. The prize is worth 12 million Swedish crowns (about $1.2 million).

The 76-year-old was taken by surprise by the news. Permanent secretary Ingrid Carlberg said Carson told her she was in Iceland, "listening to the wind". Thirteen years after Alice Munro, Carson becomes the second Canadian to receive the honour. A classicist and translator of Greek literature, Carson is known for genre-defying works such as the verse novel "Autobiography of Red" (1998) and the poem "Nox", printed on one continuous sheet of paper in homage to her dead brother. The Academy said almost all her literary production had centred on "the intersection between the philologist's obsession with words and the poet's desire for new meaning".

## A much-predicted pick

Carson, who holds both Canadian and Icelandic citizenship (the latter since 2022), had been mentioned as a possible laureate for years. She earned three degrees from the University of Toronto and taught at McGill, the University of Michigan and Princeton. With her selection, the Academy continues its recent pattern of alternating male and female laureates following the 2018 crisis that reshaped the institution. She follows Wednesday's chemistry laureates Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai; the Nobel Peace Prize is announced on Friday, October 9.
