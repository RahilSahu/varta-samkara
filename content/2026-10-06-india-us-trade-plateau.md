India-US Trade Deal Talks Hit a "Plateau", Says Nirmala Sitharaman; Further Concessions Now Difficult

Negotiations for the India-US trade deal have reached a "plateau", beyond which further demands and concessions will be very difficult, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on Monday. Speaking at the Munich Security Conference, she described the agreement under discussion as "hard and very rigorously negotiated" and said both sides would need to find any remaining room to operate. The US has imposed 50 percent tariffs on India, while fresh concerns have emerged over proposed tariffs of up to 100 percent on major buyers of Russian energy.

## The minister's remarks

"The agreement negotiations are still ongoing, although we'd like to believe that both the sides have reached a plateau beyond which giving or taking might be very, very difficult. But maybe if there are rooms to operate from, both sides would do it," Sitharaman said. She noted that the predominant feature of India-US trade relations is the trade balance in India's favour, with the US trade deficit with India at around $34 billion in FY26, and said tariffs, market access and the broader trade imbalance remain key issues. Her remarks followed USTR Jamieson Greer's statement last week that a deal was not "imminent", with both sides identifying a set of sticking points.

## EU FTA on track

Sitharaman said India's imports of Russian oil did not feature as a condition in the negotiations for its free trade agreement with the European Union, which she said is set to be signed in December this year. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal has been in the US since September 29 for the G20 trade ministers meeting and bilateral trade negotiations.
