IISc Review Flags Flaws in Centre's Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan, Recommends Scrapping It

A technical review by the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru, has raised serious questions about the Centre's Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan, the city ranking carried out under the National Clean Air Programme, saying it measures administrative compliance rather than actual air quality and urging the Union environment ministry to scrap the framework.

## What the review found

The Department of Civil Engineering's report, "Analysis of the Framework of Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan", points to a disconnect between the scores awarded and ground reality. Lucknow, one of the top performers in the 2025-26 survey, scored nearly 198 out of 200 points even as it recorded a "very poor" AQI of 374 in December 2025. Prof Ashish Verma, Convenor of the IISc Sustainable Transportation Lab, said the rankings focus on actions taken and relative change in PM10 rather than the Air Quality Index, which creates a false perception among residents about the air they breathe.

## Conflicts of interest and missing data

The review says urban local bodies act as implementers, data collectors, self-assessors and prize recipients, with no independent field verification, which undermines the credibility of the exercise. It also notes that data collated by the Central Pollution Control Board, which functions under the same environment ministry, was not used in the analysis. The framework excludes agricultural residue burning, secondary PM2.5 formation and precursor gas monitoring despite their health impacts, and its mark allocation across industrial emissions, road dust and bundled categories penalises industrial towns while benefiting other cities, the researchers said.

## What the researchers recommend

The team has written to Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav recommending that the ministry consider scrapping and rolling back the current framework. If the ministry wishes to retain it, the letter suggests renaming it the "Swachh Vayu Action Plan Implementation Ranking" and developing a new framework featuring third-party field audits, satellite data cross-checks and citizen participation, with ambient AQI performance carrying at least 50 per cent weight. The Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan was introduced to evaluate how cities implement clean air measures under the National Clean Air Programme, which covers 131 non-attainment cities.
