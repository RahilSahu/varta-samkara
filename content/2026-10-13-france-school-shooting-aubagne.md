14-Year-Old Detained After Shooting at French Middle School Leaves Principal and Teacher Wounded

French police have detained a 14-year-old boy suspected of opening fire with a shotgun at his middle school in the southern town of Aubagne on October 12, 2026, wounding the school principal and a teacher in what prosecutors described as a rare school shooting in France.

The boy fired multiple shots while moving across two floors of the Joseph Lakanal middle school in Aubagne, about 15 kilometers east of Marseille, according to the Marseille prosecutor's office. The principal and the teacher were wounded in the legs by pellets used in 12-gauge shotguns and were taken to hospital with injuries that were not life threatening. Police said a third person was grazed in the shooting.

## Staff Member Disarms Suspect

A staff member disarmed the boy in a stairwell before police arrived and took him into custody, an act prosecutors praised for extreme courage. Mayor Jean-Pierre Squillari told reporters the teen was detained by the staff member before officers reached the scene.

Deputy prosecutor Audrey Jouaneton said the pupil fired once or twice on the first floor, wounding the principal and teacher, then went to the second floor and fired twice more, once at the floor in front of a teacher and once inside a classroom, hitting the teaching board. No teachers or pupils were hit by those shots, though those present were badly shaken.

## Officials Respond

The boy's motives remain unknown, Jouaneton said, adding that investigators did not yet have precise evidence on whether he intended to target specific staff members. Mental health counseling was offered to pupils.

French President Emmanuel Macron praised the school staff's composure and exemplary response, saying it had prevented an even worse outcome. Education Minister Edouard Geffray called the act unacceptable, and the prime minister's office said the interior and education ministers were heading to the scene.

Al Jazeera reported there was no immediate information linking the shooting to nationwide student protests over better-resourced schools that have been shaking France for several weeks. Gun violence by pupils in French schools is rare; in 2017, a 16-year-old opened fire at a high school in southern France, wounding three students and the principal.
