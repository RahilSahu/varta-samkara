Andhra Pradesh Cabinet Clears Mishra Commission Recommendations on BC Quota After SC Setback

The Andhra Pradesh Cabinet on Saturday approved the recommendations of the Rajiv Ranjan Mishra Commission on reservations for Backward Classes (BCs) in rural and urban local bodies, and decided to set up a dedicated mechanism to gather the data needed to implement the quota and take the process forward. The decision came a day after the Supreme Court dismissed the state's plea against the High Court order striking down its move to provide 34 per cent reservation to BCs in local body elections.

The special Cabinet meeting was held at the A.P. Secretariat in Velagapudi on Saturday morning. Based on proposals from the Backward Classes Welfare Department, the government passed a resolution clearing the commission's recommendations, which fixed BC reservations after accounting for SC and ST quotas within applicable legal ceilings and judicial guidelines. The Panchayati Raj and Municipal Administration departments will now issue fresh Government Orders finalising the quota, and local body elections will be conducted based on these orders.

## Supreme Court upholds High Court order

Hearing the state's special leave petition on Friday, October 9, a Supreme Court Bench of Justices Dipankar Datta and Sheel Nagu dismissed the appeal, describing the High Court's order as based on a "very reasonable approach". The High Court had set aside G.O. Nos. 105 and 1065, through which the government sought to provide 34 per cent reservation to BCs in Panchayat Raj institutions and 33.33 per cent in urban local bodies, holding that the quotas breached the 50 per cent ceiling laid down in the 1992 Indra Sawhney judgment and lacked empirical justification.

Appearing for the state, senior advocate Mukul Rohatgi contended that the quota was determined afresh on the basis of a statewide survey, verification by gram and ward sabhas, and the findings of an independent dedicated commission. The Bench remained unconvinced, observing that data drawn from a unified family survey was insufficient to justify the enhanced quota without a specific empirical assessment of backwardness and representation in local bodies.

## Naidu promises 34 per cent at party level

On Friday, Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu, addressing a public meeting in Kondapi, Prakasam district, said the NDA government had made efforts to ensure 34 per cent reservations for BCs. He recalled that N.T. Rama Rao had introduced 24 per cent reservation for BCs in local bodies and said he had subsequently increased the quota to 34 per cent, alleging that the previous YSRCP government had reduced it to 24 per cent and legally challenged the enhanced quota.

Naidu announced that 34 per cent of local body seats from the NDA alliance would be given to BC candidates at the party level through TDP nominations. In a separate ruling, a three-judge Bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant said local body elections could not be delayed on the ground that the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls was under way, and existing rolls should be used if the revised rolls were not published before the election notification.
