India Tells UN It Will Not Bow to Nuclear Blackmail in Reply to Pakistan

India exercised its Right of Reply at the UN General Assembly First Committee in response to remarks by Pakistan, saying nuclear weapons must not be treated as routine instruments of statecraft and that New Delhi will not bow to nuclear blackmail. India Permanent Mission to the UN shared the remarks on X on October 11.

First Secretary Anmol Sher Singh Bedi said India military posture is defensive and shaped by its own security needs, not directed against any specific state. He described India response after the Pahalgam terror attack, which killed 26 civilians, as measured, targeted and non escalatory self defence.

## Three points stated by India

Mr Bedi set out three points: any terrorist attack against India will receive a fitting response, India will not bow to nuclear blackmail, and India will make no distinction between sponsors of terrorism and those who mastermind it. He said using non state actors does not erase a state responsibility or dilute India right of self defence.

He also said escalation concerns should be judged by conduct over rhetoric, and that relying on a nuclear overhang to limit consequences of sub conventional activity raises escalation risk rather than restraint. India said it does not seek conflict and continues to pursue peace, but without accountability, peace becomes only a pause between crises.

In a separate exchange on minority rights accusations, India said Pakistan lectures others on rule of law while at home the army is the rule and the law, according to reports of the UN remarks.
