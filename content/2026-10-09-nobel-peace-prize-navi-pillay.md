South African Jurist Navi Pillay Wins 2026 Nobel Peace Prize for Work on International Law

The Norwegian Nobel Committee on Friday awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, the 85-year-old South African jurist, "for her efforts to promote peace and international law".

"The system of international law is under tremendous pressure, and its institutions are under attack," the committee said, describing Pillay as a lawyer and legal pioneer who held states and leaders accountable. Born in Durban to a family of Indian Tamil origin, Pillay became the first woman to open a law practice in Natal in 1967 and the first non-white woman on South Africa's High Court. She defended Nelson Mandela and other anti-apartheid activists, served on the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and the International Criminal Court, and was UN High Commissioner for Human Rights from 2008 to 2014. She chaired the UN commission of inquiry that concluded last year that Israel committed genocide in Gaza, a finding Israel called scandalous.

The prize, worth 12 million Swedish crowns (about $1.2 million), will be presented in Oslo on December 10. The committee received 287 nominations this year. Reacting, Pillay said she would dedicate the award to survivors of international crimes, and joked she might share it with President Trump. Israel's foreign ministry denounced the award as "the grotesque weaponisation of an award to legitimise anti-Israel hatred". Hours later, Trump said the committee had "demeaned" the prize and the White House imposed fresh sanctions on the International Criminal Court.
