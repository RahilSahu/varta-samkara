Hurricane Isaias Strengthens to Major Category 3 Storm Ahead of Gulf Coast Landfall

Hurricane Isaias strengthened into a major hurricane on Friday morning as it moved toward the US Gulf Coast, with the National Hurricane Center reporting maximum sustained winds of 120 mph based on data from an Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter aircraft.

Landfall is expected tonight near Pensacola, Florida, along the Alabama-Florida border, with tropical storm conditions stretching from New Orleans to the Tallahassee area and tropical storm watches extending inland to Birmingham, Alabama. The storm is forecast to bring life-threatening storm surge of up to 9 feet from the Alabama-Florida border to Grayton Beach, Florida, damaging winds, 4 to 8 inches of rain with local totals up to 15 inches, and a risk of tornadoes across the Southeast through Saturday. Isaias is the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season and the first to strike the US since Hurricane Milton two years ago.

States of emergency have been declared in parts of Alabama, Florida and Georgia, with mandatory evacuation orders in two Florida Panhandle counties. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis told a press briefing: "The resources have been flowing, the preparations have been extensive and we are taking this storm very, very seriously." The Alabama-Georgia college football game scheduled for Saturday night in Tuscaloosa will proceed, with officials monitoring conditions. At least two northwestern Florida airports were set to suspend operations, and the storm has disrupted oil and gas production in the Gulf of Mexico.
