Kenya Traces Contacts of First Imported Ebola Case, WHO Commends Rapid Response

Kenya has launched an extensive contact-tracing operation after confirming its first imported case of Ebola virus disease, identifying 57 contacts, of whom 10 have been placed under quarantine. The patient, a Kenyan citizen, died at Nairobi Hospital on October 5, two days before laboratory tests confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola virus disease.

The patient had lived in the Democratic Republic of Congo for seven years, where he fell ill about a month ago and received treatment at several hospitals, Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale said. He later travelled by road from the DRC to Kampala, Uganda, then boarded Jambojet flight 8523 from Kampala and arrived in Nairobi on October 3. At Jomo Kenyatta International Airport he was taken to Nairobi Hospital, where doctors isolated him after assessing his symptoms and travel history.

## Tracing passengers, crew and health workers

The contacts identified so far include family members of the deceased, attending health workers and immigration officials, with teams still working to reach additional passengers: 23 passengers and four crew members who shared the Kampala-Nairobi flight, Duale said. Quarantined contacts are being monitored daily for symptoms under arrangements made by the Ministry of Health.

A total of 276 samples have been tested across five laboratories, including Kenya Medical Research Institute facilities in Nairobi and Kisumu, the national virus reference laboratory, and two mobile laboratories deployed at the Busia border and in Bungoma County. Only one sample, belonging to the deceased patient, has been confirmed positive.

## "Safe, open and vigilant"

Duale stressed that the imported case does not indicate widespread transmission. "Kenya remains safe, open and vigilant," he said, adding that the government was responding based on verified epidemiological information and established public health protocols. He also assured Kenyans and the international community of the country's preparedness, citing 4,971 health workers trained on Ebola response and screening of more than 652,000 travellers at 15 points of entry.

The World Health Organization backed Kenya's handling of the case. Director-General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said Kenya acted quickly after the patient's arrival and confirmed the case to WHO in line with International Health Regulations, commending the government "for its prompt action and for reporting the case to WHO and the International Health Regulations in a timely way."

President William Ruto ordered the National Security Council to convene and assess the situation, directing continued contact tracing and expanded readiness. Kenya has established dedicated isolation, treatment and quarantine facilities at Kenyatta National Hospital, Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital, the National Police Service Hospital, Port Reitz Hospital and The Nairobi Hospital, with a combined capacity of 241 beds, and has placed travellers arriving from 10 countries under enhanced surveillance.
