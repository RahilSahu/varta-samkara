Houthis Fire Missiles at Aden Presidential Palace and Airport, Five Killed

The Iran-backed Houthi group has carried out missile strikes on the presidential palace in the southern Yemeni city of Aden, killing five people and wounding 16, followed a day later by a strike on the airport grounds.

## The palace strike

On Thursday, the Houthis hit the Al-Maashiq Presidential Palace with five missiles, according to Yemeni Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani. Health Minister Qasem Buhaibeh said the dead and wounded included guards and civilian employees. Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani, who was present at the palace, was unharmed.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the group had struck what he called the "Saudi military operations headquarters" in Aden.

## The airport strike

On Friday, two missiles landed on the grounds of Aden International Airport as a Yemenia passenger plane prepared to take off for Cairo. No casualties were reported. The airport is next to a military site, and the Houthis said they were targeting a military headquarters and weapons depot.

## Wider context

The attacks come as Yemen's war, largely frozen under a 2022 truce, has reignited and become another front in the wider regional conflict involving Iran. The UN Security Council has condemned the Houthis' continued attacks on Saudi Arabia and on commercial shipping in the Red Sea.
