Maharashtra Sets Up Kakodkar-Led Panel to Assess Data Centre Environmental Impact

The Maharashtra government has constituted a high-level committee chaired by former Atomic Energy Commission chairman Dr Anil Kakodkar to comprehensively study the environmental, infrastructural and socio-economic impacts of data centres across the state, issuing a Government Resolution late on Monday night.

## Why the panel was formed

The move follows a calling attention motion raised by members of the Maharashtra Legislative Council under Rule 101 during the 2026 Monsoon Session. Lawmakers flagged the heavy demands data centres place on electricity, water, land and existing infrastructure, alongside concerns over temperature rises, noise pollution, environmental degradation and effects on the daily lives of local residents. Industries Minister Uday Samant had assured the legislature that a committee of domain experts would be constituted.

## Who is on the committee

The panel brings together senior state bureaucrats, public utility chiefs, academics, researchers and industry leaders. Members include the Additional Chief Secretary (Energy); the Principal Secretaries of the Water Resources, Industries-Energy-Labour, and Environment and Climate Change departments; the CEO of the Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation; the Commissioner (Industries); and the chairmen and managing directors of MSEDCL and MSETCL. The technical and academic members are Dr S. Venkat Mohan, Director of CSIR-NEERI, Nagpur; Prof M.V. Rane of IIT Bombay; Prof Dr Shamita Kumar of Bharati Vidyapeeth, Pune; and Prof Dr Shyam R. Asolekar of Preventive Environmental Management Techno-Legal Consultants, Mumbai. Industry is represented by Srinivasa P. of Yotta Data Services and Prasad Kalyanraman of Amazon Web Services. The Commissioner (Services), Directorate of Services, will serve as Member Secretary.

## The mandate and the deadline

The committee will assess the scientific impact of data centres on water, energy, air, noise and local ecosystems, study domestic and international best practices for energy-efficient and green operations, and recommend water conservation, recycling and alternative cooling technologies. It has also been asked to investigate and address public misconceptions about data centres, formulate communication frameworks for villagers, local self-government bodies and public representatives, recommend a standardised operating procedure for data centres, suggest cross-departmental administrative improvements, and propose measures for local employment, skill development and corporate social responsibility. The panel must submit its final report within three months, with the MIDC, the Directorate of Services and the Directorate of Industries providing administrative, technical and financial support.
