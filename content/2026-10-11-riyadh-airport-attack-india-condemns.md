12 Killed and 309 Injured in Attack on Riyadh Airport, India Condemns Strike

An attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday killed 12 people and injured 309 others, Saudi Arabia General Authority of Civil Aviation said early on Sunday, October 11. Airport operations were temporarily suspended pending damage assessment and safety checks.

The authority said the dead included four Saudi citizens and eight foreign residents: two from Bangladesh and one each from the United States, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine and Sudan. The injured were treated at several hospitals, with some in serious condition, it said.

Reports described the strike as the third attack on the airport this week and the deadliest in Saudi Arabia in more than a decade. A Saudi led coalition blamed Yemen Houthis and described the attack as a terrorist act, while the Houthis warned airlines and travellers to avoid Saudi airports except Makkah and Madinah, and said they were ready for reciprocal measures to keep civilian facilities outside the confrontation.

## India and US responses

India Ministry of External Affairs condemned the repeated attacks on the airport, including the October 10 attack, saying attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure are completely unacceptable and must cease immediately. India said it stands in solidarity with the people and government of Saudi Arabia and hoped peace and normalcy return to the region at the earliest.

US President Donald Trump said the attack was not acceptable and that he would speak with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attacks and expressed solidarity with Saudi Arabia. Saudi and Houthi forces continued exchanging strikes, with Houthi run media reporting Saudi strikes on Sanaa airport and other areas, figures that could not be independently verified.
