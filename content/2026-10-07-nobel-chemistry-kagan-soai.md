Kagan and Soai win 2026 Nobel Chemistry Prize for solving "mirror molecule" mystery

Scientists Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai have won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced in Stockholm on Wednesday. The work solves a puzzle that has challenged chemists for more than a century: how homochirality, the dominance of one of two mirror-image forms of a molecule in living systems, can emerge.

Many molecules essential to life exist in two mirror-image forms, called chiral forms, like left and right hands. Living organisms almost always use only one version. Kagan took the first decisive step in 1986 when he discovered a way to tilt chemical reactions toward producing far more of one mirror image than previously thought possible. Soai took the research further and in 2003 designed the first chemical reaction in which only one of the two possible mirror images was formed, showing that homochirality could be created chemically, not only by living organisms.

## Why it matters for medicines

The committee said the discoveries have been decisive for chemists who design reactions to manufacture pharmaceuticals. Many drug molecules occur in two mirror-image variants, where one has the therapeutic effect and the other can cause unwanted or even harmful side effects.

"Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai have provided a solution to a chemical mystery that is over a century old: how homochirality can emerge spontaneously," said Heiner Linke, chair of the Nobel Committee for Chemistry. Kagan, 95, of France's Universite Paris-Sud, and Soai, 76, of the Tokyo University of Science, will share prize money of 12 million Swedish kronor, about $1.2 million.
