Rajnath Singh Launches Navy's First Indigenous Fleet Support Ship 'Surya' in Vizag

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh on Tuesday launched the Indian Navy's first indigenously built Fleet Support Ship, FSS-1, christened 'Surya', at Hindustan Shipyard Limited (HSL) in Visakhapatnam. The vessel received traditional honours, performed by Laila Swaminathan, president of the Navy Wives Wellness Association.

On the same occasion, Singh laid the keel of the fifth ship in the series, FSS-5, and inaugurated the shipyard's upgraded Slipway No. 4, a 235-metre long and 37.5-metre wide facility, along with a 300-tonne Goliath crane. The ceremony was attended by Chief of the Naval Staff Admiral Krishna Swaminathan, Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh and HSL Chairman and Managing Director Rear Admiral C. Raghuram, among others.

## The Fleet Support Ship programme

FSS-1 is the first of five 43,500-tonne Fleet Support Ships being built by HSL for the Indian Navy under a contract signed in August 2023. The programme uses an indigenous design with 82 per cent indigenous content and integrated modular construction, and is expected to deliver its first vessels from mid-2027.

The ships are designed to replenish deployed warships at sea with fuel, water, ammunition and stores, allowing the fleet to remain at sea for longer periods without returning to port. Key features include enhanced cargo and fluid carrying capacity, at-sea replenishment capability, first-line maintenance workshops, a helicopter deck for multi-role helicopter operations and self-defence systems.

## Expanded shipbuilding capacity

The upgraded Slipway-4 and the 300-tonne Goliath crane expand HSL's covered building dock capacity to 80,000 DWT and enable the yard to build and launch naval platforms of up to 45,000 DWT. HSL said the upgrades will significantly increase its ability to take on large and complex naval and commercial platforms.

Addressing the gathering, Singh said keel laying was a symbol of resolve and the new slipway a symbol of growing capacity, adding that the multiple developments at one location on one occasion underlined India's expanding maritime power. He also linked the modernisation of naval shipbuilding to the country's long maritime tradition.
