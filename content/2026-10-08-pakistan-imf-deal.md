IMF Reaches Staff-Level Deal with Pakistan, Potentially Unlocking $1.21 Billion

The International Monetary Fund has reached a staff-level agreement with Pakistan on reviews of some of its lending programs, potentially unlocking about $1.21 billion in financing pending approval by the IMF Executive Board, the fund said on Wednesday. An IMF team led by Iva Petrova held talks in Karachi and Islamabad from September 23 to October 7 as part of the 2026 Article IV consultation.

Upon approval, Pakistan would access about $1 billion (SDR 760 million) under the Extended Fund Facility and about $210 million (SDR 154 million) under the climate-focused Resilience and Sustainability Facility, bringing total disbursements under the two arrangements to about $5.7 billion. The agreement covers the fourth review of the 37-month EFF and the third review of the 28-month RSF.

## Growth, reserves and the fuel subsidy question

The IMF said Pakistan had successfully navigated the impact of the Middle East conflict, with real GDP growth reaching 4 per cent in the first three quarters of FY26 and easing to an estimated 3.6 per cent for the full year. Headline inflation eased to about 10.3 per cent in September after peaking in May, while gross reserves rose to about $21.5 billion by end-September. The fund said the State Bank of Pakistan should maintain an appropriately tight monetary stance. Separately, Pakistan agreed to immediately scrap its Rs 75 billion cheap-petrol scheme for motorcycles and small cars after the IMF objected to the untargeted subsidy, according to reports from Islamabad.
