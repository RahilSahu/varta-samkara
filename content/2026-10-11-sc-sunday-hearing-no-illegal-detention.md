Supreme Court in Sunday Hearing Says Peaceful Protesters Must Not Be Illegally Detained

The Supreme Court held a special hearing on Sunday, October 11, and directed the Union government and the Delhi Police to ensure that people taking part in peaceful gatherings are not detained except through the procedure established by law. The bench said free movement of people and traffic, and normal functioning of Metro and rail services, should be facilitated, subject to public order.

A bench of Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana heard urgent applications alleging violation of the court order dated October 9 and large scale detentions in Delhi on October 10 during protests seeking the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. The court described the sitting as extraordinary and exceptional.

## Notices and directions

The bench issued notices to the Union Home Secretary, the Delhi Police Commissioner and the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation in contempt proceedings linked to alleged non compliance with earlier directions on transport restrictions. It asked Solicitor General Tushar Mehta to ensure that Metro services run on their normal schedule and that lawyers, journalists and others are not obstructed in their professional work.

Petitioners told the court that Metro services had not functioned normally, that New Delhi and Hazrat Nizamuddin railway stations had remained closed, that traffic advisories were not issued, and that people were detained even beyond areas covered by prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023. Mr Mehta told the court that entry and exit restrictions on Saturday applied at 45 of 303 Metro stations.

## Context of the protests

The hearing followed a day of mass detentions in Delhi as police stopped a planned gathering at Jantar Mantar by the Cockroach Janta Party, INDIA bloc parties and student groups. Senior lawyers compared the restrictions to the Emergency period, a characterisation the government has rejected. The court also said restrictions on assembly and movement must meet the conditions in Article 19(2) of the Constitution and asked protesters to maintain peace.
