INS Samarthak Commissioned at Karwar, First Private-Built Warship Joins Indian Navy

Samarthak, India's first multi-purpose vessel built by a private shipyard, was commissioned into the Navy on Friday at the Naval Base at Karwar, Karnataka, marking a major milestone for domestic warship construction.

## The ceremony

The commissioning ceremony was attended by Vice-President C P Radhakrishnan and Chief of the Naval Staff Admiral Krishna Swaminathan. The vessel, built by Larsen and Toubro (L&T) at its Kattupalli shipyard in Tamil Nadu, has more than 75 per cent indigenous content and was designed, engineered and built entirely in-house at L&T's Warship Design Centre and shipyard. "Samarthak is the first major or big warship contracted to and constructed by a private shipyard to be commissioned into the Indian Navy," Admiral Swaminathan said.

## Roles and capabilities

The vessel is designed for a range of operational and support roles, including trials of indigenous weapons and sensors, operations involving unmanned autonomous systems, and maritime surveillance and patrol duties. It is equipped with a state-of-the-art dynamic positioning system, heavy-towing facilities, medical infrastructure and firefighting capabilities, and can support humanitarian assistance and disaster relief operations as well as non-combatant evacuation missions. It is the first of four multi-purpose vessels to be inducted, under a Rs 887 crore Defence Ministry contract signed with L&T in March 2022.
