Villages Evacuated as Flash Flood Hits Budhi Gandaki River in Northern Nepal

Residents along the Budhi Gandaki river in north central Nepal evacuated on Sunday, October 11, as flash flooding destroyed homes and other structures, the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority said. The agency warned people to move to safer areas and remain on alert until late Sunday.

The Associated Press reported, citing the disaster authority and the Nepal Army, that at least five people were reported missing in one account, while The Hindu reported no immediate reports of casualties in its early coverage. The flood damaged or destroyed four bridges, swept away about three dozen houses and damaged a hydropower plant construction site, AP reported. Power and communication towers along the river were also damaged.

## Cause and rescue

AP reported that the flood was caused by a landslide near Chumchure village that initially blocked the river, forming a dam that later breached. The Nepal Army said it had rescued 49 people by Sunday afternoon using helicopters and ground troops.

The Budhi Gandaki merges into the Trishuli river and is a key waterway near Kathmandu. Videos on social media showed houses and sheds being swept away, and warnings prompted evacuations in Arughat, a riverside town on a highway connecting Kathmandu to other parts of the country. The exact number of affected villages was not immediately clear.

Nepal is still recovering from a devastating flood in August along the Nepal China border region, according to reports.
