RBI Raises Repo Rate by 25 Basis Points to 5.50%, Markets Slide

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) on Wednesday raised the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent and shifted its stance to "calibrated tightening", marking the first rate hike since February 2023 and a reversal of the four rate cuts totalling 125 basis points delivered in 2025.

Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the MPC voted unanimously after assessing evolving macroeconomic and financial conditions. The central bank raised its FY27 CPI inflation forecast to 5.2 per cent from 5.0 per cent, while revising the GDP growth projection upward to 7.1 per cent from 6.7 per cent, signalling resilient growth alongside emerging price pressures.

Indian equities turned volatile and traded lower after the announcement. By 2.10 pm, the Sensex was down 574.51 points or 0.79 per cent at 72,493.30, while the Nifty 50 fell 214.60 points or 0.94 per cent to 22,561.50. Banking stocks recovered partially after an initial dip, with the Nifty Bank index turning marginally positive, but concerns over a higher-for-longer rate environment, elevated crude prices and rupee weakness kept broader sentiment under pressure.

The rupee depreciated 22 paise to 96.57 against the US dollar in early trade after the decision. Analysts said the shift in stance effectively rules out rate cuts for now, with the next policy action likely to be either another hike or a pause depending on inflation data. Economists noted that the hike was largely priced in by markets, and the RBI's willingness to act could reinforce confidence in its commitment to price stability amid rising global yields and a continuing West Asia crisis.
