124 Former Officers Defend Election Commission and SIR in Open Letter

A group of 124 former IAS, IFS, IPS and allied services officers has issued an open letter to citizens defending the Election Commission of India and the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, and questioning allegations of vote rigging made without credible evidence. The letter was reported on Saturday, October 10, amid protests seeking the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.

The signatories said a charge made at press conferences but never placed on oath can be neither tested nor answered, and that suspicion is not proof. They said they belong to no party and had prepared rolls, trained polling parties and supervised counts during their service.

## Safeguards and past results cited

The letter pointed to safeguards including polling agents, EVM sealing, strong room security and VVPAT verification. It cited the 2025 Bihar Assembly election, which it said recorded the highest turnout since 1951 and no re poll, and noted that booth capturing was once so common that Parliament made it a crime in 1989.

The officers also noted that under the same Commission, the ruling BJP lost Assembly elections in Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Telangana, Jharkhand and Jammu and Kashmir, and lost its Lok Sabha majority in 2024. The winners raised no complaint, the letter said.

## Signatories

Named signatories in reports include former foreign secretary Kanwal Sibal, former IAS officers R. D. Kapur, Samirendra Chatterjee, Gopal Krishna, Vivek Bhardwaj and Navneet Sehgal, former IFS officers Bhaswati Mukherjee and Prabhu Dayal, and former IPS officers K. Aravinda Rao and Sarvesh Chandra. The group included former secretaries, ambassadors, directors general of police and chief secretaries.

Opposition parties and protesters have maintained that the SIR has removed eligible voters and have demanded the CEC resignation, a claim the Commission and the government have rejected.
