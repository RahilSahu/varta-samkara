Hackers Heat French Supermarket Fridges to 50C in Large-Scale Cyberattack, Sparking Fires and Spoiling Food

Hackers raised the temperature of refrigerators and freezers at hundreds of French supermarkets to as high as 50 degrees Celsius (122 degrees Fahrenheit), causing fires and spoiling food in what the Paris prosecutor's office has called a large-scale attack.

The Paris prosecutor's office said on October 12 that the attack was affecting connected refrigerators at several major food retailers across France, leading to food losses and fires. The attackers remotely pushed refrigeration units up to 50C for about six hours overnight, forcing stores to discard large amounts of spoiled food.

## Hundreds of Stores Affected

Les Mousquetaires, owner of retailer Intermarche, said fridges at around 150 of its stores were heated up to 50C overnight, and a spokesperson said all affected food had been discarded to eliminate health risks. Carrefour said 80 of its supermarkets were hit, while Monoprix said about 5 percent of its roughly 490 stores were affected. Cooperative U CEO Dominique Schelcher confirmed his supermarket group had also been targeted, and E.Leclerc said several of its stores were hit.

Intermarche CEO Thierry Cotillard told BFM TV the unprecedented attack would represent losses amounting to tens of millions of euros nationwide in a single day, according to CNN. Cooperative U's Schelcher wrote on X that targeting equipment essential to preserving fresh products was of extreme gravity and unprecedented.

## Government Launches Investigation

Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu instructed France's National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI) to investigate, a person in his office said. France was coordinating with European counterparts because other countries could also be affected, and authorities were assessing whether other French companies were at risk.

A source close to Lecornu said ANSSI had identified a vulnerability in software supplied by Danish company Danfoss, which makes refrigeration management systems used by the stores. Danfoss said it was aware of incidents in France affecting a limited number of refrigeration management systems, and that the affected units were running outdated firmware. The company said it had long advised customers to keep supported systems updated and to migrate legacy systems to current-generation solutions.

Cybersecurity firm Claroty's Team82 published research on August 9 detailing vulnerabilities in the Danfoss AK-SM 800A controller, reporting that attackers could have bypassed authentication controls and executed code on compromised devices. French retail association Federation du Commerce et de la Distribution called for full transparency from Danfoss about the incident and said supermarkets aimed to protect consumers and return to normal operations as soon as possible.

## Wider Impact

Felix Aime, a senior cyber threat intelligence analyst at Sekoia Threat Detection and Research, said Spain, Portugal, Poland, Germany and Belgium had also been affected. Separately, Dutch retailer Ahold Delhaize said it was dealing with a security issue on its network and had taken several systems offline as a precaution, though the company said there was no reason to see a link between the incidents in France and its supermarkets.

The attack underlines the importance of resilience across the entire supply chain, said Christel Delberghe, director general of European retail association Eurocommerce.
