Panama Hit by 7.7-Magnitude Earthquake, Its Strongest in More Than 70 Years

A 7.7-magnitude earthquake struck central Panama on Friday afternoon, collapsing buildings and roads in the country's central provinces and prompting evacuations across the Central American nation. President Jose Raul Mulino said no deaths had been documented as of Friday evening, while authorities reported at least 27 minor injuries.

The United States Geological Survey said the quake struck at 1756 GMT (12:56 pm local time) with an epicentre about 10 kilometres southwest of the small town of Pitaloza Arriba in central Herrera province, at a depth of around 10 kilometres. The USGS initially estimated the magnitude at 8.0 before revising it to 7.7. The agency called it the strongest earthquake to hit that part of Panama since at least 1951, and an early USGS estimate put potential economic losses above $1 billion.

## Damage in central provinces, capital rattled

The shallow tremor lasted for around a minute and was felt across the nation of about 4.5 million people, and as far away as neighbouring Colombia, according to the National Civil Protection System. In Panama City, about 200 kilometres from the epicentre, residents evacuated high-rise buildings in panic. The head of emergency operations, Rene Garcia, said there were no reports of fatalities, while civil protection said 27 people sustained minor injuries.

Damage was concentrated in the central provinces of Veraguas, Herrera and Los Santos. In Penonome, a cathedral in the district's main square partially collapsed, the Dos Continentes hotel was damaged, and the colonial-style Church of San Atanasio in Los Santos province, declared a National Historic Heritage Site in 1938, collapsed, local outlets reported.

## Response and aftershocks

"We are facing a major disaster," President Mulino said, adding that some roads had "completely collapsed." He travelled to Penonome to coordinate the response and called for calm, cautioning that the casualty count could change in the coming hours or days.

The quake was followed by a magnitude-6.6 aftershock, and more than 80 aftershocks were recorded. The Panama Canal Authority said shipping through the channel was unaffected and operating as normal. Tocumen International Airport in Panama City briefly suspended operations but reopened on Friday afternoon. A tsunami warning issued for the eastern Pacific was later lifted.

Regional leaders offered support: US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Washington was "rapidly mobilizing resources and personnel to assist" at Panama's request, and the presidents of Colombia, Chile, Guatemala, Uruguay, Costa Rica and Ecuador, as well as Venezuela's acting president, expressed solidarity.

Source: Associated Press, Reuters, AFP
