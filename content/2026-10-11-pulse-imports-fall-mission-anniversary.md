Pulse Imports Fall 31 Percent as Aatmanirbharta Mission Completes First Year

India pulse imports fell by 31.52 percent in value in 2025-26, while domestic production rose, according to an official factsheet issued on Sunday, October 11, marking the first anniversary of the Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses. The Press Information Bureau backgrounder said the mission was launched on October 11, 2025, with an allocation of Rs 11,440 crore for 2025-26 to 2030-31.

Total pulse production rose to 274.09 lakh tonnes in 2025-26 from 256.83 lakh tonnes in 2024-25. Cultivation area expanded from 277.20 lakh hectares to 286.46 lakh hectares, and yield rose from 926 kg per hectare to 957 kg per hectare, the factsheet said.

Imports declined from Rs 46,427.60 crore in 2024-25 to Rs 31,793.59 crore in 2025-26. Exports rose 13.4 percent from 855 million dollars to 969.53 million dollars, with total export volume of 10.01 lakh tonnes. Major destinations included China, the UAE, Bangladesh, the USA and Sri Lanka.

## Targets to 2030-31

By 2030-31, production is expected to reach nearly 350 lakh tonnes with yield of 1,130 kg per hectare. The mission focuses on tur, urad and masoor, promotes climate resilient seeds, and plans distribution of 87.5 lakh free seed kits and 126 lakh quintals of certified seeds over six years.

It follows a cluster based approach covering 489 districts, plans 1,000 processing units, and provides assured procurement of tur, urad and masoor through NCCF and NAFED from registered farmers. Major pulse growing states listed include Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Jharkhand, Odisha and West Bengal. India is the world largest producer of pulses, which contain 20 to 25 percent protein, PIB said.
