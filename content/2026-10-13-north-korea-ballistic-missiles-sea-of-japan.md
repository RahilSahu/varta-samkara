North Korea Fires Multiple Ballistic Missiles Toward Sea of Japan, One Travelling Over 900 Km

North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles toward the Sea of Japan on October 12, 2026, with at least two believed to have fallen into waters outside Japan's exclusive economic zone, according to the Defense Ministry in Tokyo.

The ministry said it detected two separate launches from North Korea's eastern coast. The first missile, fired at around 9:19 a.m., traveled about 220 km, reaching a maximum altitude of 20 km. The second missile, launched around 9:36 a.m., flew more than 910 km, reaching a maximum altitude of 70 km. Both missiles splashed down in the Sea of Japan, outside the exclusive economic zone, which extends 200 nautical miles (370 kilometers) from Japan's shores. Japan's Kyodo News reported that South Korea's military detected several ballistic missiles of different types launched near Pyongyang at around 9:20 a.m.

## Regional Response

Japanese parliamentary vice defense minister Takashi Koyari said Japan had lodged a protest with North Korea through a diplomatic channel and strongly condemned the launches for contravening relevant United Nations Security Council resolutions. He said authorities were analyzing details, including whether the missiles flew on irregular trajectories. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi instructed government offices to gather and analyze information, share it quickly and accurately with the public, and ensure safe navigation at sea and in airspace.

A South Korean military source said the launches were a response to Seoul's first test-firing of a hypersonic missile earlier in the month, Kyodo reported. South Korea's National Security Office convened an emergency security meeting following the launch to review the country's military readiness.

## Rising Tensions on the Peninsula

The launches came amid weeks of mounting friction between the two Koreas. South Korea said on October 3 that North Korea had fired a ballistic missile that Pyongyang described as an intermediate-range strategic missile capable of flying on an irregular trajectory, according to the Korean Central News Agency. The Manila Bulletin, citing Jiji Press, reported that the latest firing also followed a land mine explosion along the heavily fortified border that injured three South Korean soldiers, a matter over which Seoul and Pyongyang have traded accusations.

Japan and South Korea are both allies of the United States, and North Korea's repeated missile launches and nuclear weapons development have prompted closer bilateral and trilateral cooperation.

Source: The Japan Times, The Mainichi (Kyodo), Reuters
