US pulls B-1 bombers from UK base on "credible" Iran threat as Rubio says Tehran lost Hormuz control

The United States has abruptly withdrawn about a dozen B-1 long-range bombers from RAF Fairford in Britain after a "credible threat" from Iran that potentially involved a drone attack, a US official told NPR on Wednesday. The move comes in the eighth month of the US-Israel war with Iran, which began on February 28.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Wednesday that Iran had "lost complete control" of the Strait of Hormuz and that Tehran's economy was in "total and complete freefall," according to AFP. Iranian officials have said the US maritime and air blockade is having a severe effect on the economy.

## Warnings on both sides

Senior Israeli defence officials told the Security Cabinet that Iran could miscalculate and launch missiles at Israel amid the pressure, the Jerusalem Post reported. Iran's Defence Ministry spokesman Reza Talaei-Nik warned Tuesday that any renewed attack would be met with a "decisive response," saying retaliatory targets were prepared and that weapons production capacity had increased 2.5-fold compared with before the war.

President Donald Trump said on Monday that high oil prices were "a small price to pay" for keeping Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons, and that he believed the war could end soon. Brent crude futures traded above $100 a barrel on Wednesday as investors weighed higher Gulf exports against continued supply risks, with Reuters reporting markets remain nervous about further attacks on ships.

Source: NPR, The Jerusalem Post, Dawn
