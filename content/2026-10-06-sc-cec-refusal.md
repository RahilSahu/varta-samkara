Supreme Court Refuses Interim Suspension of CEC Gyanesh Kumar, Seeks Replies Within a Week

The Supreme Court on Monday refused to pass an interim order suspending Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, allowing him to continue in office while it examines allegations that key decisions during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls were taken without the concurrence of the two other Election Commissioners. A three-judge bench led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana, issued notices to the Centre, the Election Commission of India, the CEC and the two Election Commissioners, seeking replies within a week.

## What the petitions allege

The petitions, filed by Rakesh Kumar Singh, Shailendra Mani Tripathi and Sudhakar Singh, allege that around 14 SIR-related decisions were taken without the consensus of Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi. The pleas followed an Indian Express investigation reporting that the two commissioners had recorded objections at least 14 times between October 2025 and August 2026 to decisions and orders on the SIR issued without their knowledge or recommendation. Senior advocate Vikas Singh, appearing for one of the petitioners, sought the CEC's suspension or the divesting of his powers pending the hearing, calling it a grave threat to electoral democracy.

## What the court said

The bench declined to act on the basis of media reports alone. "We cannot pass an order on the basis of a news report," CJI Surya Kant said, adding, "If there is any violation, we will undo." When Singh pressed for suspension, the bench questioned the practical effect: "Who will conduct the elections?" In a connected plea argued by senior advocate Menaka Guruswamy on modifications to statutory Form 6, the CJI made it clear that the court had not approved any changes: "We didn't approve modified Form 6, let's be very clear." This addressed the ECI's September 26 statement that the Form 6 declaration for the SIR had been "upheld" by the Supreme Court.

## ECI's response

The Election Commission of India has rejected the charge of wrongdoing, maintaining that its decisions were taken unanimously. The court has asked the poll body, the Centre and the others to place on record the relevant official documents and respond within a week.
