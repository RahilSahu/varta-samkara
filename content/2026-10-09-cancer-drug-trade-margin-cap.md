Centre Caps Trade Margins on Non-Scheduled Anti-Cancer Drugs at 30% of MRP

The central government has approved a 30 per cent cap on trade margins charged on the supply and sale of non-scheduled anti-cancer medicines. The move is expected to reduce prices of covered brands by 20 to 70 per cent and save patients an estimated Rs 2,500 crore annually.

## Targeting 170 Per Cent Average Mark-ups
The decision follows an analysis by the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA), which found that non-scheduled anti-cancer medicines carry an average price mark-up of approximately 170 per cent, with some cases reaching 700 per cent or more. The Department of Pharmaceuticals said prices also vary sharply between retail pharmacies, hospital pharmacies and online pharmacies. The cap, set at 30 per cent of MRP (equivalent to 42.86 per cent of the price to stockist), targets distributor, retailer and hospital mark-ups rather than manufacturers' prices.

## Expert Panel to Finalise List
An expert committee under the Directorate General of Health Services will finalise the list of medicines to be covered, after which the NPPA will issue the implementing notification. The measure extends a February 2019 pilot that capped margins on 42 selected anti-cancer drugs, which the government says cut MRPs by up to 91 per cent and saved Rs 984 crore across 526 brands. The intervention comes after the Supreme Court sharply criticised the high mark-ups, with one bench describing them as "broad daylight dacoity". The matter is before the court again on October 12. To avoid shortages, manufacturers must maintain current production levels.
