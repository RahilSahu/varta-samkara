Flydubai Co-Pilot Planned Hijack Attack Before Joining Airline, Probe Finds

The flydubai co-pilot who stabbed his captain on a flight to Israel last week had planned the attack before being hired by the airline, according to officials familiar with the investigation. He told UAE interrogators that he deliberately sought work at airlines that operate regular flights to Tel Aviv.

The co-pilot, an Omani national identified by sources as Hamam al-Hammami, was in custody in the UAE. The UAE prosecutor general has described the incident as a "terrorist attack". No formal charges had been announced, and it was not known whether he had appointed legal counsel.

## What happened on flight FZ1073

The co-pilot attacked the captain of flydubai flight FZ1073, which was bound for Israel from Dubai, with an emergency crash axe, according to the UAE prosecutor general. The aircraft was escorted and diverted, landing safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. Flydubai later suspended all flights to and from Israel pending the investigation.

## The pre-planning

A third source briefed on the probe said the co-pilot had planned to crash Wednesday's flight into Ben Gurion airport near Tel Aviv. The same source said he had also operated a flight to Tel Aviv earlier this year and intended to hijack and crash that plane as well, but abandoned the plan at the last moment. It was not immediately clear why he changed his mind, though the source said he is believed to have known someone aboard that earlier flight.

Israeli officials, speaking on condition of anonymity, said Israel was being continuously updated about the investigation by the UAE. Neither UAE nor Israeli authorities, nor flydubai, immediately responded to requests for comment on the latest disclosures.

## Australia link under scrutiny

A multi-agency Australian counterterrorism unit is investigating the co-pilot's links to Australia after reports that he studied at a university in Melbourne about a decade ago. RMIT University confirmed that a person matching the account had been enrolled there but did not successfully complete an engineering qualification. Australian police said the Victorian Joint Counter Terrorism Team, which includes federal police, state police and the ASIO intelligence agency, was investigating the man's links to the country.
