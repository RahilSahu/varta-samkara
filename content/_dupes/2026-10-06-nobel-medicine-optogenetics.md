Deisseroth, Hegemann and Nagel win 2026 Nobel Prize in Medicine for optogenetics

Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel have won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for their "discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics", the Nobel Assembly at Sweden's Karolinska Institutet announced on October 5.

Deisseroth, 54, is an American professor at Stanford University, while Hegemann, 71, is associated with Humboldt University of Berlin and Nagel, 73, with the University of Würzburg in Germany. The trio will share prize money of 12 million Swedish kronor (about $1.2 million).

## Switches made of light

Optogenetics lets scientists switch the activity of individual nerve cells in a living brain on or off using light. The story began in the 1990s, when Hegemann, then at the Max Planck Institute for Biochemistry, studied how a single-celled alga, Chlamydomonas, swims towards light. He identified a light-sensitive protein, and working with Nagel in the 2000s, the pair showed the protein, channelrhodopsin, acts as a switch that opens an ion channel when exposed to light.

Deisseroth then took the discovery further. He genetically engineered nerve cells to produce channelrhodopsin, and his 2005 experiments showed that shining light on them triggered nerve signals, laying the foundation for the technique now used in laboratories worldwide to map which cells are responsible for which function. Researchers can identify and even control neural circuits behind specific memories, feelings and behaviours relevant to neurological and psychiatric disorders.

Committee member Anna Wedell said the work has opened "a completely new dimension of understanding of the function of the brain", adding that it is only the beginning. The technique has also produced clinical results: in 2021, a blind patient with retinitis pigmentosa partially recovered vision through an optogenetic treatment.
