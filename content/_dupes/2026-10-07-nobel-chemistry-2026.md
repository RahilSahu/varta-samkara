Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai win 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for mirror-molecule breakthroughs

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry has been awarded to French chemist Henri B. Kagan and Japanese scientist Kenso Soai for solving a century-old puzzle about how mirror-image molecules form. The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced the award in Stockholm on Wednesday, recognising the pair "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis."

Many molecules exist in two forms that are mirror images of each other, like left and right hands. In living organisms, only one form appears, a phenomenon called homochirality, but lab reactions long produced an equal mix of both. Kagan took the first decisive step in 1986 when he developed a method to steer reactions toward producing more of one mirror image.

Soai built on that foundation. In 2003, he created the first chemical reaction that produced only one of the two possible mirror-image forms, showing that homochirality could arise through chemistry itself and was not unique to living things. Committee chair Heiner Linke said the pair had provided a solution to a mystery more than a century old.

## Why it matters for medicine

The discoveries have shaped how pharmaceuticals are manufactured. Many drugs are chiral, meaning only one mirror image has the desired therapeutic effect while the other may be inactive or even harmful. The reactions developed by Kagan and Soai have been decisive for chemists designing manufacturing processes, and the underlying principles have found uses in agriculture and materials as well.

Kagan, born in the Parisian suburb of Boulogne-Billancourt in 1930, is affiliated with Universite Paris-Sud in Orsay, France. Soai, born in Hiroshima in 1950, is based at the Tokyo University of Science. They will share the prize money of 12 million Swedish kronor, worth roughly 1.2 million dollars.

The chemistry prize follows the medicine prize, announced Monday, and the physics prize, announced Tuesday. The literature prize is set for Thursday, the peace prize for Friday, and the economics prize for Monday.
