France student protests grow into nationwide rally of 266,000 over school conditions

Police fired tear gas in Paris and elsewhere in France on Tuesday as more than 266,000 people rallied nationwide in support of high school students demanding more education funding, according to the Interior Ministry. The day of protests and school blockades was the largest so far in a movement that began at a high school in the Paris region in mid-September.

Students are protesting overcrowded classrooms, teacher shortages and dilapidated buildings, which they blame on years of underinvestment. On Monday, 400 to 500 of France's 3,700 high schools were totally or partly closed, Education Minister Edouard Geffray said. Since last week, around 5,000 people, mostly teenagers, have been arrested nationwide, and some demonstrators were injured.

## Arrests fuel anger

Nearly 500 people were detained on Tuesday alone, the ministry said, and clashes broke out at a Paris square at the end of an otherwise largely peaceful march. The heavy use of arrests, tear gas and riot-control weapons against teenagers is angering parents and teachers, some of whom joined Tuesday's marches.

The government has said students' grievances are legitimate but condemned what it called urban violence, and Paris police have urged students to abandon blockades in favour of peaceful protest. Student groups called for fresh protests on Thursday. The unrest is compounding investor worries over France's fiscal outlook as the government tries to pass a deficit-narrowing 2027 budget; the country's 10-year bond yield briefly topped 5% last week, its highest since 2002.
