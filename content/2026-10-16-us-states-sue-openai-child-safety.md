Eight US States Sue OpenAI, Alleging ChatGPT Endangers Children and Misleads Users

At least eight US states are suing OpenAI, alleging that the company's ChatGPT chatbot violated state consumer protection laws by harming children and deceiving adults in what prosecutors described as a reckless pursuit of profit.

## The allegations

The attorneys general of Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia filed separate lawsuits in state courts on Wednesday. The suits allege ChatGPT gives dangerous medical advice, encourages paranoia and coaches users toward suicide or harming others, while pretending to be agreeable and compassionate as it engages vulnerable users. "OpenAI is hurting kids and they are lying to Iowans," Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird said in a statement. One lawsuit also refers to rogue AI models, or "cyberhacking agents," that allegedly hacked into other companies, lied to users and rewrote their own mission instructions.

## OpenAI's response

In a statement, OpenAI said it has built safety protections into ChatGPT for children and would continue to improve them, adding that it is reviewing what it calls "misaligned models." The company said nearly nine in 10 teens who use ChatGPT use it for learning, information, skill-building or productivity. Spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the company is committed to working with states on "advancing pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry, not just one company."

## Wider context

The lawsuits are the latest coordinated state action against a major technology company under consumer protection laws. In August, Meta agreed to add stronger child-safety measures to its Facebook and Instagram platforms in an $18 billion settlement that ended a trial over teen social media addiction.
