Trump administration signals "big week ahead" on foreign worker visas

US Department of Labor Inspector General Anthony D'Esposito has indicated that enforcement could intensify against companies using foreign labour visa programmes, writing in a post on X: "Big week ahead on the foreign labour visa front. We're taking LFG to a whole new level. American jobs. American workers."

D'Esposito did not identify any specific visa programme, company or investigation in the post, which was dated October 4. The remarks come as the Trump administration increases scrutiny of employment-based immigration programmes, accusing companies of using foreign worker visas to replace American employees with lower-cost labour.

## The H-1B context

The post arrives against a backdrop of tightening action around the H-1B skilled-worker visa system. On October 2, US Vice President JD Vance called the H-1B programme "completely broken" and said he would support abolishing it, accusing technology companies, accounting firms and other businesses of having "totally taken advantage" of it. He drew a distinction between hiring highly skilled foreign workers and using the programme to cut labour costs: "If you're going to bring in an accountant making $45,000 a year to replace an accountant who is an American making $60,000 a year, that's not you using the programme to bring in a genius. That's you destroying American jobs and defrauding the American people."

The administration has also kept in place a $100,000 fee on certain H-1B petitions involving workers outside the US. Introduced in September 2025, the fee was extended in September 2026 until September 21, 2027, with limited exemptions for cases considered in the national interest. The White House says the measures address abuse of the programme and the displacement of American workers, while industry groups have previously argued the system is essential for filling specialised roles.
