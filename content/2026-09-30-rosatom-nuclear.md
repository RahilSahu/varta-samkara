Russia's Rosatom is in talks with Adani, Reliance, Jindal and NTPC to join India's nuclear power push.

Kirill Komarov, Rosatom's First Deputy Director General for Corporate Development and International Business, told businessline: "We now have talks with a lot of companies from the private sector, including all your famous names like the Adani Group, Reliance and Jindal, and your state-owned company NTPC."

The outreach follows the SHANTI Act, 2025, which opened India's nuclear sector to private participation for the first time. India aims to grow nuclear capacity from 8.78 GW today to 100 GW by 2047.

Rosatom says it reached 50% localisation at Kudankulam in Tamil Nadu and is ready to replicate that with new Indian entrants. Two Kudankulam units already operate reliably, with four more at various stages of implementation.

## A long road to 100 GW

The 100 GW target is ambitious and will need private capital, foreign technology partnerships and regulatory clarity to move together. Rosatom's pitch is that its Kudankulam track record, and the localisation it has already achieved, make it the natural partner for the next wave.
