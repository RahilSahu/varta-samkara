The Technology Development Board, under the Department of Science and Technology, signed an agreement on September 24, 2026 with Bengaluru-based GalaxEye Space Solutions to build a satellite that can see through clouds and darkness.

Under the deal, the Centre's RDI Fund will contribute Rs 63.84 crore toward the Rs 247.69 crore project. The funding is aimed at taking the technology from TRL-6, a demonstrated prototype, to TRL-9, proven in actual operation.

## OptoSAR: radar and optical on one platform

The satellite will use OptoSAR imaging, combining radar (SAR) and optical (EO) sensors on a single platform. That combination delivers resolution sharper than 0.5 metres, and critically, it works at night and through cloud cover, conditions that blind conventional optical satellites.

The planned applications include wide-area surveillance, human and vehicle detection, and target classification. If successful, the project would give India an indigenous all-weather imaging capability, reducing dependence on foreign satellite data for persistent monitoring.

The agreement is part of a broader push to channel public research funding into private space startups, as India works to build a commercial space ecosystem alongside its established state-run programme.
