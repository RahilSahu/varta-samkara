Trump Announces Energy Ceasefire Between Russia and Ukraine, Kyiv Seeks Clarity

US President Donald Trump announced on Sunday, October 11, that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an energy ceasefire effective immediately, covering attacks on each other energy infrastructure. He urged both sides not to break it in a post on Truth Social.

There was no immediate independent confirmation from Moscow or Kyiv that the agreement had taken effect. A senior Ukrainian official told the Kyiv Independent that Ukraine had no information about such a ceasefire. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told Axios that if Mr Trump had negotiated it with Russian President Vladimir Putin, Kyiv should be told when it begins, and that Ukraine is ready to accept it.

## Background

Earlier on Sunday, Mr Zelenskyy said Ukraine was prepared to halt strikes on Russian oil refineries if Moscow stopped attacking Ukrainian energy infrastructure, and that any de escalation must be reciprocal. In an ABC interview, he also said he would organise elections if Mr Putin agreed to a 60 day total ceasefire, noting that elections cannot be held under martial law imposed after Russia full scale invasion in February 2022.

The announcement followed a diesel deal announced on Friday after a call between Mr Trump and Mr Putin, under which the US planned purchases of Russian diesel. Mr Zelenskyy criticised that deal, while the Trump administration said Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries had affected global diesel supply and US prices. CNN reported that the White House and the Kremlin had been asked for details on how the energy ceasefire was reached.
