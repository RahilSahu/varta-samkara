DRDO successfully tests indigenous high-altitude airship at 21 km in the stratosphere

The Defence Research and Development Organisation on Tuesday announced the successful flight trial of its indigenously developed High-Altitude Platform System, an airship that reached 21 kilometres above mean sea level. The experimental flight remained at around 20 kilometres for more than 30 minutes before being commanded to descend, and the platform was recovered safely.

The Ministry of Defence said data collected during the mission was analysed to assess performance. Real-time video and vehicle parameters were transmitted continuously to a ground control station during the flight. The system carried an inertial measurement unit, GPS receiver, onboard cameras, an altitude-control mechanism and other electronic subsystems, all of which functioned as intended.

## Long-endurance stratospheric surveillance

The lighter-than-air platform is being designed as a long-endurance aerial surveillance system capable of operating in the stratosphere, well above conventional aircraft. It is being developed by DRDO's Aerial Delivery Research and Development Establishment in Agra. Such platforms are intended for persistent surveillance, communications and monitoring missions.

The trial was conducted in coordination with the Indian Air Force, the Centre for Military Airworthiness and Certification, the Directorate General of Civil Aviation, the Airports Authority of India and other civil authorities to ensure the flight was carried out safely.

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh congratulated DRDO, the Air Force, public sector undertakings and industry partners, describing the trial as an important milestone in self-reliance in advanced aerospace technologies.
