Ukraine scrambles for money to fight war as Russian strikes batter the economy

Kryvyi Rih, the hometown of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy, is struggling to survive. Russian airstrikes have brought the city's huge steel plant and mines to a standstill, dragging the local economy to its knees, Reuters reported on October 5.

Mayor Oleksandr Vilkul, 52, a former mining executive, said in a video address that the sprawling industrial city of about 600,000 people was doing everything possible to keep hospitals open, lights on in kindergartens and schools, and buses running. "In Kryvyi Rih, the situation is actually worse than anywhere else, apart from the front line itself," he said, adding: "It's about survival. Right now, we need to survive."

## A budget crisis at the front and at home

The financial squeeze underlines the challenges facing Ukraine as the government navigates its biggest budget crisis since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022. Last month, the city's largest employer, ArcelorMittal's mining and steelmaking complex, suspended operations after Russian ballistic missile strikes darkened its furnaces.

The scene is repeated across Ukraine's once-mighty steel industry, which accounted for a tenth of economic output before the war. Giant mills in Zaporizhzhia and other industrial cities stand silent, and exports have stopped. An escalation in Russian drone and missile strikes over the summer destroyed factories and warehouses, damaged ports and railways, and forced shops and businesses to close, slowing economic growth and tax revenue. Meanwhile, the technology-driven war is becoming ever more expensive for Ukraine to fight.

Russia has not publicly responded to the specific claims about the Kryvyi Rih strikes in the Reuters report. Moscow has consistently described its campaign as targeting Ukrainian military and industrial infrastructure tied to the war effort.
