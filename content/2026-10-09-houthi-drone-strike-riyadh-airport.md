Houthi Strikes Damage Saudia Aircraft at Riyadh Airport; Three Killed, Flights Suspended

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis escalated strikes against Saudi Arabia on Thursday, hitting Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport and damaging an aircraft belonging to state carrier Saudia, Saudi civil aviation authorities confirmed. Three Saudi nationals were killed and several people were injured.

Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation (GACA) said the first attack targeted airport facilities while the second struck a Saudia aircraft parked at the terminal. Shrapnel from an interception left the plane heavily damaged after catching fire; no passengers were believed to be on board, though injuries could not be ruled out. Several Saudi citizens and residents of different nationalities were hospitalised with injuries ranging from minor to serious. Separately, Saudi officials said debris from an intercepted missile hit a kindergarten and a medical complex in Riyadh.

The Houthis said they struck the airport and warned workers to stay away from energy plants, which they said would be targeted. They say they are targeting the kingdom over its support for government forces in Yemen's renewed civil war, while Riyadh says it backs an offensive by Yemen's UN-recognised government. Lufthansa and Air India confirmed suspension of flights to Riyadh, and aviation analytics firm Cirium said nearly half of departures had been cancelled by 1500 GMT on Thursday. The Houthis have also launched attacks on oil facilities this week.
