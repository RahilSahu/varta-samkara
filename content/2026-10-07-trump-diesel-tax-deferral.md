Trump defers tax on red-dyed diesel through year-end as US fuel prices near $6.50 a gallon

President Donald Trump has signed an executive order deferring collection of the federal excise tax on red-dyed diesel fuel through December 31, 2026, directing the Treasury Department and the IRS to implement the deferral and provide penalty relief for temporary on-road use of the fuel. Red-dyed diesel is normally reserved for off-road uses such as farming and construction and is exempt from highway fuel taxes.

Trump said the move would save truckers about $100 each time they fill up and save farmers millions of dollars, as diesel prices have climbed to around $6.50 a gallon amid supply disruptions, refinery constraints and geopolitical tensions. On Tuesday, Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds issued a proclamation allowing dyed diesel for certain agricultural hauling on public roads, effective through February 2, 2027.

## Experts question the impact

Analysts said the order's effect on prices is likely to be limited. "Taxes aren't the problem, supply is," said Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at the price-tracking site GasBuddy. The order does not abolish the tax; it only defers it, while the Treasury examines options, including legislation, to remove the deferred liability altogether.

Trump has argued that fuel prices will drop once current wars are resolved, and the Des Moines Register noted the order followed fuel price spikes linked to the US-Israel war on Iran and Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries. The administration is also sending $500 refund checks to nearly one million Affordable Care Act enrollees who paid full premiums on HealthCare.gov, with payments starting in October.
