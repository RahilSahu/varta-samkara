Single GST Registration Likely for Small E-Commerce Sellers

The GST Council is likely to consider a proposal at its October 7 meeting to allow small businesses selling goods exclusively through e-commerce platforms to operate with a single GST registration, even if their goods are stored across multiple states and Union Territories. The proposed scheme is expected to be optional.

Currently, a small seller storing goods in warehouses or fulfilment centres in multiple states may have to obtain separate GST registrations in each of those states, which requires maintaining a physical place of business in every state.

## How the scheme would work

Under the proposed framework, a supplier would need a physical presence in at least one state or Union Territory, which would be treated as the principal place of business. The supplier could then obtain a single registration against a PAN in a state or Union Territory and declare warehouses in other states as additional places of business, but only with the prior concurrence of the e-commerce operator concerned.

The scheme would apply only to sales made through e-commerce operators. If a business also wants to make supplies outside e-commerce platforms, it would have to obtain regular GST registration, including maintaining a physical place of business in each state where required.

## Safeguards and responsibilities

The proposal includes several safeguards. E-commerce operators would have to appoint an authorised representative in every state and Union Territory to provide information about registered suppliers to tax officials, inform the GST authorities if a supplier is deactivated from their platform, and assist in proceedings and recovery of tax dues.

A seller would undergo physical verification and Aadhaar authentication in one state. If an e-commerce operator withdraws its consent for a seller to use its warehouse as a place of business, the seller would get 30 days to designate another operator's warehouse for that state or exit the scheme.

There would also be a limit on input tax credit. A supplier that wishes to pass on ITC above Rs 2.5 lakh in a month to a registered person would be required to withdraw from the scheme and obtain regular registration.

## Compliance burden

The proposal is aimed at easing the compliance burden on small online sellers and could benefit around 9.5 lakh small sellers by letting them access markets across the country without setting up physical offices wherever their inventory is stored. The change would require an amendment to the GST law, and final eligibility criteria and operational conditions would depend on the Council's decision.
