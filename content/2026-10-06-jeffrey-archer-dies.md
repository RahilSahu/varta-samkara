Jeffrey Archer, best-selling novelist and former politician, dies aged 86

Jeffrey Archer, the best-selling British novelist and former politician who was jailed for lying in a high-profile libel case, has died aged 86, his publisher HarperCollins said on October 5.

Archer died suddenly and peacefully at his home "between writing sessions" on Monday, according to the publisher. He sold over 300 million copies of his books worldwide and was known for pacy thrillers including his 1976 debut Not a Penny More, Not a Penny Less and the 1979 bestseller Kane and Abel. His final novel, Adam and Eve, was published this month, a day after his death, and he had been due to discuss it at the Cheltenham Literature Festival this weekend.

## Writer and politician

Before becoming a bestselling novelist, Archer had a prominent political career. He became a Conservative MP in 1969, later served as deputy chairman of the Conservative Party under Margaret Thatcher, and was made a life peer in 1992 as Lord Archer of Weston-super-Mare.

His political career was overshadowed by scandal. In 1987, Archer sued the Daily Star tabloid for saying he had paid a woman for sex, winning a then-record 500,000 pounds in damages. A former friend later accused him of lying, and in 2001 he was convicted of perjury and perverting the course of justice, sentenced to four years in prison, and served two. He was expelled from his party, returned to the House of Lords after his release, and retired in 2024.

Kate Elton, chief executive of HarperCollins UK and Archer's editor, described him as "the master storyteller and an editor's dream", adding: "We're all devastated to have lost a writer who has brought joy to generations of readers, and a true friend."
