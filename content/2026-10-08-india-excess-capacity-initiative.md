India Joins 15-Economy US-Led Initiative Against Manufacturing Overcapacity

India has joined a 15-country US-led initiative to tackle "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing, signing a joint ministerial statement alongside the US and 13 other economies in Washington on the sidelines of the OECD Trade Committee meeting on October 7. The other signatories are Argentina, Australia, Canada, the EU, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Poland, Turkiye and the UK.

The initiative will initially cover automobiles and electric vehicles, batteries, chemicals, foundational semiconductors and solar panels. The signatories will establish sectoral platforms to exchange information and explore coordinated action, committing to meet at the technical level before December 2026 to draft terms of reference, share non-confidential data on excess capacity and identify gaps using OECD work. The statement calls on countries to end "non-market policies and practices" that distort markets, without naming any country, and invites other economies to join.

## India's balancing act amid Section 301 scrutiny

The move comes about seven months after the USTR launched a Section 301 investigation into structural excess capacity across 16 economies, including India, with final findings due March 11, 2027. The USTR has flagged Indian solar-module capacity as nearly three times annual domestic demand, alongside concerns in steel and petrochemicals. Experts say New Delhi must balance cooperation with its own manufacturing ambitions; the government has maintained that Indian capacity is geared to meet both domestic and global demand.
