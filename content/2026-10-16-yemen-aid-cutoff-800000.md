Yemen Fighting Cuts Aid to 800,000 People and Overwhelms Hospitals, Agencies Warn

Intensified fighting in Yemen has cut off life-saving humanitarian assistance to around 800,000 people, while front-line hospitals are struggling to cope with a sharp rise in war casualties, humanitarian agencies said on Wednesday.

## Aid access blocked

The UN World Food Programme said fighting along multiple front lines has made several districts inaccessible because of fighting and road closures, with wheat flour stocks nearly depleted and remaining food in local markets expected to last only a short time. Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city and the area hosting the largest number of people fleeing the fighting, is among the worst affected. "This is the kind of situation that can rapidly spiral into a humanitarian catastrophe if assistance cannot move safely and reach those who need it most," said El-Khidir Daloum, WFP Representative and Country Director in Yemen.

## Hospitals under pressure

The International Committee of the Red Cross said front-line hospitals in areas including Marib, Taiz and Yemen's Red Sea coast are seeing unprecedented numbers of weapon-wounded patients, with some facilities admitting more than 700 casualties in a single week. "Trauma care staff are under enormous pressure," ICRC health coordinator Feras Abusamra said, warning that hospitals face shortages of surgical supplies, anaesthesia, oxygen and medicines.

## Displacement grows

More than 200,000 people have been displaced from their homes since fighting began in recent weeks, according to the UN's International Organization for Migration. The renewed fighting is between the Iran-backed Houthi group and Saudi-backed government forces.

Source: Reuters, UN World Food Programme
