GST Council Scraps Tax Officers' Arrest Powers, Raises Prosecution Threshold to Rs 5 Crore

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council on Thursday approved a series of compliance-easing reforms at its 57th meeting, scrapping the arrest powers of GST officers and raising the prosecution threshold to Rs 5 crore from Rs 1 crore, the Finance Ministry said. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced the decisions after the meeting, adding that GST rate changes will now be taken up once a year as the rate structure stands settled.

Under the revised framework, cases involving amounts below Rs 5 crore will not attract prosecution, the minimum punishment has been removed, and the decision on whether a case warrants a fine, imprisonment or both is left to judicial discretion in every case. The general penalty, applicable where no specific penalty is prescribed, has been reduced from Rs 25,000 to Rs 10,000. The ministry said improved GST data analytics, which match invoices issued by sellers with those reported by buyers, have made it possible to identify fraudulent input tax credit closer to its source.

## Easier e-commerce registration and ITC relief

The Council approved a simplified GST registration mechanism for small sellers operating through e-commerce operators, allowing them to sell across states without establishing a place of business in each state. It also decided to set up a Committee of Officers to examine how to protect genuine buyers who hold proper invoices, have received the goods and paid their suppliers in full, but lose input tax credit when a supplier elsewhere in the value chain defaults. The committee will complete its study within three months for the next Council meeting. Reuters noted the changes respond to long-standing complaints from businesses about stringent tax practices.
