The Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission (UPPSC) Combined State/Upper Subordinate Services Examination, called UPPSC PCS, selects officers for posts like Deputy Collector, DSP, Tehsildar, and Block Development Officer. It runs in three stages: Prelims (screening), Mains (descriptive, merit), and Interview.

Source note: the official syllabus PDF on uppsc.up.nic.in could not be opened directly during research (the site's syllabus download page returned an error), so this page follows the syllabus as published in the UPPSC PCS notifications and reproduced by standard exam references. Always verify the paper list, marks, and any rule changes against the latest official notification before you apply.

Exam Stages at a Glance

| Stage | Papers | Marks | Duration | Nature |

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Prelims | Paper I: General Studies | 200 (150 questions) | 2 hours | Objective, merit for Mains shortlisting |

| Prelims | Paper II: CSAT (aptitude) | 200 (100 questions) | 2 hours | Objective, qualifying only (33%) |

| Mains | General Hindi + Essay + GS I to VI | 1500 total | 3 hours each | Descriptive, counts in final merit |

| Interview | Personality Test | 100 | As scheduled | Counts in final merit |

Final merit is based on Mains (1500) plus Interview (100), a total of 1600 marks. Prelims marks are not carried forward.

Prelims: Paper I (General Studies)

Paper I has 150 objective questions for 200 marks, so each question carries 1.33 marks. The official syllabus lists these broad areas:

- Current events of national and international importance: recent major events, government schemes, policies, and contemporary issues.

- History of India and Indian national movement: ancient, medieval, and modern Indian history, the freedom struggle, its leaders, and key events.

- India and world geography: physical, social, and economic geography of India and the world, natural resources and their distribution, population, and human geography.

- Indian polity and governance: the Constitution, political system, Panchayati Raj and local self-government, public policy, and rights-related issues.

- Economic and social development: sustainable development, poverty and social inclusion, demography, social sector initiatives, and contemporary economic issues.

- Environment and biodiversity: general topics on ecology, biodiversity, climate change, and environmental conservation. No subject specialisation is required.

- General science: basic concepts of physics, chemistry, and biology, plus everyday applications of science and technology.

Special note from the notification: candidates are expected to study all these subjects with special reference to Uttar Pradesh, so UP-specific facts carry heavy weight in Paper I.

Prelims: Paper II (CSAT)

Paper II has 100 objective questions for 200 marks, 2 marks each, with a 2-hour duration. It is qualifying in nature: you must score at least 33 percent (66 out of 200) to be eligible for Mains. Its marks do not count in the Prelims merit. Topics:

- Comprehension

- Interpersonal skills, including communication skills

- Logical reasoning and analytical ability

- Decision making and problem solving

- General mental ability

- Elementary mathematics up to Class X level: number systems, averages, ratios, percentages, profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed and distance, algebra, geometry, and statistics

- General English up to Class X level: comprehension, voice, parts of speech, sentence transformation, direct and indirect speech, punctuation, spellings, vocabulary, idioms, and fill in the blanks

- General Hindi up to high school level: grammar, sandhi, samas, antonyms, synonyms, idioms and proverbs, spelling, and UP dialects

Mains: General Hindi (150 marks)

Paper I of Mains is a 3-hour descriptive paper in Hindi for 150 marks. It is not a qualifying paper: its marks count in the final merit. Topics:

- Comprehension of prose passages, with questions and answers

- Precis writing (summary of a passage)

- Official and semi-official letter writing, drafting of office orders, notifications, and circulars

- Word knowledge and usage: prefixes and suffixes, antonyms, one-word substitutions, spelling, and sentence correction

- Idioms and proverbs (muhavare and lokoktiyan)

Mains: Essay (150 marks)

Paper II is a 3-hour descriptive paper for 150 marks. Candidates write essays from three sections, each covering a theme group:

- Section A: literature and culture, social sphere, political sphere

- Section B: science, environment, and technology; economic sphere; agriculture, industry, and trade

- Section C: national and international events, natural calamities, national development programmes and projects

Essays can be written in Hindi or English. The sectioned format means you should prepare at least one strong essay angle on culture, economy/science, and current affairs each.

Mains: GS Paper I (200 marks)

History, culture, geography, and society. 3 hours, descriptive.

- Indian art, literature, and architecture from ancient to modern times, with contributions of prominent dynasties and cultural movements

- Modern Indian history: key events from 1757 to 1947, British colonial policies, Indian resistance, and socio-economic change

- The freedom struggle: its phases, major events, and leaders such as Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, and Subhash Chandra Bose

- Post-independence consolidation: political and social integration, reorganisation of states, major policies up to 1965

- World history: major events from the 18th to the mid-20th century, including the French Revolution, Industrial Revolution, the World Wars, and ideologies like socialism, Nazism, and fascism

- Indian society and culture: social structure, role of women, caste dynamics, population issues, poverty, and urbanisation challenges

- Effects of economic reforms (liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation) on Indian economy and society

- Physical geography: Earth's structure, tectonics, natural disasters, climatic zones, and ecosystems

- Oceanic resources, marine exploration, and India's policies

- Human migration: causes, effects, and refugee issues with focus on India

- Population and settlements: demographic trends, urban-rural patterns, and smart cities

Mains: GS Paper II (200 marks)

Polity, governance, social justice, and international relations. 3 hours, descriptive.

- Indian Constitution: historical background, evolution, salient features, amendments, and the role of the Supreme Court in constitutional interpretation

- Functions of the Union and the states, federal structure, division of powers, and centre-state relations

- Finance Commission: role in fiscal federalism and devolution of funds

- Separation of powers, judicial independence, and alternative dispute resolution

- Comparison of the Indian Constitution with the USA, UK, and Canada

- Parliament and state legislatures: functions, powers, privileges, and legislative procedure

- Executive and judiciary: ministries, departments, PILs, and pressure groups

- Representation of People's Act: election processes and eligibility rules

- Constitutional posts: President, Governor, Election Commission, CAG, and others

- Statutory and regulatory bodies: NITI Aayog, UPSC, Finance Commission, and others

- Government policies in health, education, infrastructure, and ICT

- Role of NGOs, SHGs, and community organisations in development

- Welfare schemes for women, children, SCs, STs, and other vulnerable sections

- Social sector issues: education, health, human resource development, and skill development

- Poverty and hunger: causes, government interventions, and global indices

- Governance: e-governance, transparency, and accountability

- Role of civil services in democracy

- International relations: India's foreign policy, neighbours, SAARC, BRICS, G20, and strategic partnerships

- Policies of developed and developing countries and their impact on India, Indian diaspora

- International institutions: UN, WTO, IMF, World Bank, and other agencies

Mains: GS Paper III (200 marks)

Economy, technology, environment, security, and disaster management. 3 hours, descriptive.

- Economic planning in India: five-year plans, their achievements, and the role of NITI Aayog

- Poverty and unemployment: causes, types, regional disparities, and government measures for inclusive growth

- Government budgets: budgeting process, types of budgets, fiscal policy, and taxation

- Agriculture: major crops, cropping patterns, irrigation systems, agricultural marketing, and e-technology

- Farm subsidies, MSP, PDS objectives, and food security

- Food processing industry: significance, infrastructure, supply chain, and government initiatives

- Land reforms after independence and their effects

- Liberalisation and globalisation: industrial policy changes and effects on growth and trade

- Infrastructure: energy, transport, communication, and urban infrastructure

- Science and technology: advances, applications in daily life, and role in national security

- Achievements of Indians in science and technology, indigenisation, and dual-use technologies

- ICT and space technology: missions, satellites, and digital initiatives

- Environmental security: ecosystem conservation, wildlife, biodiversity, and pollution control

- Disaster management: types of disasters, mitigation, national policies, and institutions

- International security: nuclear proliferation, extremism, cyber security

- Internal security: terrorism, insurgency, organised crime, and the role of law enforcement agencies

- Mandate and organisation of the armed forces, paramilitary, and intelligence agencies

- Issues in agriculture, horticulture, forestry, and animal husbandry

Mains: GS Paper IV (200 marks)

Ethics, integrity, and aptitude. 3 hours, descriptive. Includes case studies.

- Ethics and human interface: essence, determinants, and consequences of ethics in human actions

- Human values: lessons from leaders, and the role of family, society, and educational institutions in building values

- Attitude: content, structure, and function; moral and political attitudes and their influence on behaviour

- Aptitude for civil services: integrity, impartiality, non-partisanship, objectivity, dedication to public service, and empathy

- Emotional intelligence: concepts, dimensions, and use in administration

- Moral thinkers and philosophers from India and the world, and how their teachings apply today

- Public service values: ethical concerns in governance, laws, accountability, and ethical governance

- Probity in governance: RTI, transparency, codes of conduct, citizens' charter, and corruption

- Case studies applying ethical principles and governance issues to real situations

Mains: GS Paper V (200 marks)

Uttar Pradesh: history, culture, and society. 3 hours, descriptive. This UP-specific paper replaced the old optional subjects.

- History and culture of UP: historical contributions, ancient cities, architectural heritage, museums, and archaeology

- UP's role in the freedom struggle before and after 1857, and eminent freedom fighters from the state

- Social issues in UP: rural, urban, and tribal issues, festivals, music, folk dances, languages, and customs

- Political system of UP: Governor, Chief Minister, State Assembly, State Council, and centre-state relations

- Public service in UP: functions of the Public Service Commission, auditing, Advocate General, and High Court jurisdiction

- Local self-government: urban bodies and Panchayati Raj institutions in UP

- Good governance in UP: anti-corruption measures, Lokayukta, citizen charters, e-governance, and RTI

- Land reforms in UP: historical and current effects on development

- Security issues in UP: links between development and extremism, internal security, and communication networks

- Medical and health issues: healthcare challenges, state policies, and infrastructure

- Education system of UP: contribution to national development, current issues, and reforms

- Welfare schemes: implementation and impact of central and state schemes in UP

- Tourism in UP: prospects, heritage, tourism policy, and economic impact

Mains: GS Paper VI (200 marks)

Uttar Pradesh: economy, geography, and environment. 3 hours, descriptive.

- Economy of UP: main features, state budgets, infrastructure, and resource management

- Trade and commerce: industrial policies, trade dynamics, economic zones, and investment

- UP government schemes: welfare projects, human resource, and skill development

- Investment in UP: issues, impacts, public finance, and economic reforms

- Agriculture in UP: commercialisation, crop production challenges, agro-based industries, and forestry

- Geography of UP: physical features, climate, irrigation, minerals, drainage, and vegetation

- Pollution and environmental issues in UP: policies, the Pollution Control Board, and mitigation

- Natural resources of UP: soil, water, air, forests, grasslands, wetlands, and conservation

- Science and technology in UP: advances, issues, and government promotion of innovation

- Public-private partnerships: development initiatives and their economic impact

Interview: Personality Test (100 marks)

Candidates who clear Mains are called for the interview. It tests general awareness, intelligence, character, communication, and overall suitability for public service. Expect questions on your background, current affairs, and UP-specific issues. The final selection list is prepared from Mains plus Interview marks, and selected candidates must also pass the prescribed medical tests.

Marks and Qualifying Rules

- Negative marking in Prelims: one-third of the marks assigned to a question are deducted for every wrong answer (so about 0.44 marks per wrong answer in Paper I and 0.67 in Paper II).

- CSAT is qualifying only: score at least 33 percent (66 of 200) in Paper II; its marks never count in any merit list.

- Prelims merit for Mains shortlisting is based on Paper I only, and Prelims marks are not added to the final score.

- General Hindi in Mains is not a qualifying paper: its 150 marks count in the final merit, like every other Mains paper.

- Mains papers can be answered in Hindi or English. In case of any dispute over a question's meaning, the English version is treated as final in Prelims.

- There is no negative marking in Mains. All 8 Mains papers are compulsory.

- Word limits per answer are set on the question paper itself and vary by question, so check the instructions printed on your paper; the notification does not fix a single word limit for all answers.

- Eligibility basics: a bachelor's degree from a recognised university, and the standard age band is 21 to 40 years with relaxations for reserved categories. Confirm the exact age limits and reservation rules in the notification for your attempt year.
