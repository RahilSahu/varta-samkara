Propaganda, Power and the Mahatma: The Documented Record on Gandhi

Propaganda is so deeply ingrained in society that a child molester and racist is considered the "Father of the Nation," and speaking the truth about him can make you a criminal or an anti-national. Even people who are fully aware of his misdeeds avoid criticizing him because the propaganda is so deeply rooted in society.

This is not hatred. This is the documented record: his own writings, his own letters, dates, and events. Read it, check the sources at the end, and then decide for yourself.

The brahmacharya experiments

He slept and bathed with his grandniece Manu Gandhi, who was 18-19 at the time, and with other young girls, naked, to practice his own celibacy. He called these his brahmacharya experiments. The idea was to prove he had conquered lust by placing himself in the most tempting situation possible.

Where is the consent? These were young women in his ashram, under his total authority, told that this was spiritual discipline. Manu Gandhi described in her biography that the experience disturbed her mentally. His own associates were horrified. Sardar Patel and others confronted him. He did not stop.

A man who sleeps naked beside teenage girls and calls it a spiritual test would be called a predator in any other context. Here he is called the Father of the Nation.

The Kallenbach letters

Gandhi and Hermann Kallenbach lived together for periods in South Africa, exchanged many personal letters, and Gandhi referred to Kallenbach as his "soulmate." Some lines from his letters:

"How completely you have taken possession of my body. This is slavery with a vengeance."

"I would not exchange you for him. You still remain the dearest and nearest to me..."

Your portrait (the only one) stands on my mantelpiece in my bedroom. The mantelpiece is opposite to the bed.

The British-made Mahatma

He came to India in 1915 when Britain was involved in WWI, and became rapidly famous. This is not possible if the British don't support you, because the media was under British control.

He asked Indians to support Britain in WWI. In 1918 he personally recruited combatants for the British army. The apostle of non-violence raised soldiers for the empire's war.

Ask yourself: did Gandhi's non-violence favour the British or Indians? If the British were so nice, then why did they capture us? Why did they loot us? Why did the Great Bengal Famine happen? What happened at Jallianwala Bagh?

India's population in 1910 was 30 crore. The total British in India were only 2 lakh. Two lakh people ruled thirty crore for a century. That is only possible when the ruled are taught that resistance itself is immoral. Gandhi's non-violence persuaded Indians to not use violence against the British. That is exactly what the British needed.

The "simple life" myths

He used to drink goat milk. But those goats were washed with human soap and were fed dry fruits.

He travelled in general coach. But the whole coach was booked just for him.

The loincloth was a costume. The comfort never left.

Three betrayals: Bhagat Singh, the 55 crore, and the bhajan

Gandhi could have stopped the hanging of Bhagat Singh by refusing to attend the meeting organised by Viceroy Irwin unless the hanging was stopped. He went to the meeting anyway. Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were hanged on 23 March 1931.

Gandhi went on fasting and blackmailed the Indian government to release Rs 55 crore to Pakistan, when Pakistan's army was attacking Kashmir. In January 1948, while Indian soldiers were dying in Kashmir, the cabinet released Rs 55 crore owed to Pakistan during his fast at Birla House.

Gandhi changed a Hindu bhajan and added the "Ishwar Allah tero naam" line to "Raghupati Raghav Raja Ram."

The prison paradox

Why did the British never send Gandhi to Kala Pani like they did with other freedom fighters? Revolutionaries were shipped to the Cellular Jail in the Andamans to rot in solitary cells. Gandhi never was.

In fact, Gandhi had the facilities to write his own autobiography and publish his articles from jail. A jail which had a proper toilet, a window, a bed and every facility.

In August 1942, Gandhi and several of his followers, including Kasturba, were "imprisoned" by the British in the Aga Khan Palace near Pune. The Aga Khan Palace is a historic landmark built in 1892 by Sultan Muhammed Shah Aga Khan III. It served as a prison for Mahatma Gandhi and his associates during the Quit India Movement. A palace, not a prison. Kasturba Gandhi and Mahadev Desai died there.

Ask yourself why the British treated their supposed greatest enemy like an honoured guest.

The racist Mahatma

Gandhi was a racist, and the proof is in his own hand. In 1903, when Gandhi was in South Africa, he wrote that white people there should be "the predominating race." He also said Black people "are troublesome, very dirty and live like animals."

They are from his own newspaper, Indian Opinion, preserved in his Collected Works.

Africa noticed. In December 2018, a Gandhi statue was removed from a university campus in Ghana. Activists in Ghana and Malawi used the hashtag #GandhiMustFall. They were angry about his early writings, and they were right to be.

He supported the Khilafat movement, which had nothing to do with India's independence. In 1919 he joined a movement started by Muslims to protect the Islamic Caliph and save Islam after Ottoman Turkey was defeated in the Great War.

At Delhi's Valmiki Basti temple, Gandhi had the Quran read inside a Hindu temple. Hundreds of Valmiki youths stood beside the woman reader and said: before reading the Quran in the temple, read the Ramayana in a mosque, then teach us the Quran. Gandhi called the police when the protests grew. Police came and captured the protesters, a case was filed against them, and then the Quran was read at the same temple under police protection.

Nehru over Patel, and the rest

His decision to back Jawaharlal Nehru over Sardar Patel for Congress president in 1946 is considered by many to have led India down a path of superficial westernisation. Patel won the backing of 12 of the 15 Congress committees. Nehru got 0. Gandhi backed Nehru anyway. Patel withdrew. Nehru became prime-minister-in-waiting, and then prime minister.

He opposed industrialization and modernization of India.

Gandhi wrote about Hitler and the Jews, in a June 1946 interview with Louis Fischer: "Hitler killed five million Jews. It is the greatest crime of our time. But the Jews should have offered themselves to the butcher's knife. They should have thrown themselves into the sea from cliffs. I believe in hara-kiri. I do not believe in its militaristic connotations, but it is a heroic method. That would have been heroism. It would have aroused the world and the people of Germany to the evils of Hitler's violence, especially in 1938, before the war. As it is they succumbed anyway in their millions." His advice to the victims of genocide was to die more theatrically.

George Orwell wrote in a private letter on 8 April 1941: "Gandhi has been regarded for twenty years by the Government of India as one of its right hand men. I know what I'm talking about, I used to be an officer in the Indian police. It was always admitted in the most cynical way that Gandhi made it easier for the British to rule India, because his influence was always against taking any action that would make any difference."

Orwell continued: "The reason why Gandhi in prison is always treated with such lenience and small concessions sometimes made when he has prolonged one of his fasts to a dangerous extent, is that the British officials are in terror that he may die and be replaced by someone who believes less in 'soul force' and more in bombs."

An officer of the British Indian police, in writing: Gandhi made it easier for the British to rule India.

Death through ideology

In August 1942, while imprisoned at the Aga Khan Palace, Kasturba was struck with several heart attacks and suffered from poor circulation. After this ordeal, she contracted bronchial pneumonia. Their son Devadas implored Gandhi to allow her to take penicillin. Gandhi openly refused: "If God wills it, He will pull her through." She died on 22 February 1944. His ideology killed his own wife.

On 6 April 1947, Gandhi said: "If Muslims want to kill us (Hindus), we must face death bravely. Hindus should not harbor anger in their hearts against Muslims even if the latter wanted to destroy them. If they established their rule after killing Hindus we would be ushering in a new world by sacrificing our lives."

On 1 May 1947, he said: "Hindus will never be killed by Muslims in Pakistan. Hindus in Pakistan must trust Jinnah."

The question

We have been brainwashed for generations to the point that we declared such a man the "Father of the Nation." We are so deeply conditioned that even questioning his decisions instantly makes someone "anti-national." Despite well-documented facts about his life and actions, political leaders still avoid condemning him because his image is deeply embedded in our society.

History deserves questioning. Blind worship does not make a nation strong. Truth does.
