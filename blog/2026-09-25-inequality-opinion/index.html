OPINION | Wealth is concentrated everywhere.

R
Rahil Sahu
Founder, Varta & Samkara
On this page
  • The one crore thought experiment
  • Inequality is not the same as injustice
  • So what should we actually do about it

Wealth is concentrated everywhere. Not in one country, not under one system, everywhere.

Look at the top 10 percent wealth shares: USA 68 percent, China 68 percent, India 64 percent, Pakistan 59 percent, Japan 56 percent, UK 50 percent. The top 1 percent: USA 32 percent, Sri Lanka 31 percent. Capitalist America, communist China, developing India, island Sri Lanka. Different governments, different ideologies, different continents. Same pattern. (Data: World Inequality Database and World Bank Gini Index, figures as cited.)

That alone should end a certain kind of argument. If inequality appears under capitalism and under communism, in rich countries and poor ones, then it is not the product of any one system. It is a feature of money itself.

The one crore thought experiment

Try this. Give every citizen one crore rupees tomorrow. Perfect equality, overnight, no exceptions. What happens in ten years?

The money starts moving. Some people spend it, some save it, some invest it. The spender's money flows to the saver and the investor. The investor puts it into a business, the business grows, the investor now has more than one crore. The spender now has less. Within a decade, you have disparity again, created from nothing but human behavior.

That is a basic money phenomenon. Money attracts more money. The person who already has capital can take risks, absorb losses, and compound gains. The person who has nothing cannot. This is not a moral judgment on either of them. It is arithmetic.

Inequality is not the same as injustice

Here is where the conversation usually goes off the rails. Inequality existing does not automatically mean injustice exists. Every society in human history has had some people with more and some with less, because people are not identical in skill, risk appetite, discipline, or luck.

The real questions are different ones. Can a poor person rise? Is the game rigged, or just uneven? Is there a floor below which nobody falls? Those are worth fighting over. But the mere fact that the top 10 percent hold most of the wealth, that is as old as civilization and as universal as gravity.

So what should we actually do about it

If you accept that concentration is natural, you stop chasing the fantasy of perfect equality and start chasing something achievable: mobility. Make it easier for the bottom to rise. Education, credit access, property rights, the ability to start something small without drowning in red tape. Those move the needle.

And tax the right things. Nobody serious argues that billionaires should pay nothing. But there is a difference between taxing wealth to fund opportunity and pretending you can tax wealth into equality. One works. The other has been tried in various forms, in various countries, and the numbers at the top of this article show how it ends.

Wealth concentrates everywhere. The honest debate is not about whether that happens. It is about what a fair society does about it.
